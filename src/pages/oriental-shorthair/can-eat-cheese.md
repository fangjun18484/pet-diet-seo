# Can an Oriental Shorthair Eat Cheese?

**Verdict: YES (In Moderation)**

While cheese is not toxic to cats, it should be treated as an occasional treat rather than a dietary staple. Oriental Shorthairs, like most domestic cats, are obligate carnivores and do not require dairy in their diet.

---

## Why the Caution?
Most adult cats are **lactose intolerant**. Once a kitten is weaned from its mother's milk, they lose the enzyme (lactase) needed to break down the sugars in dairy. While some Oriental Shorthairs may handle cheese better than others, many will experience digestive distress.

## Potential Benefits vs. Risks

### 🟢 The Potential Benefits
*   **High Palatability:** Cheese is aromatic and tasty, making it a great high-value reward for training your intelligent Oriental Shorthair.
*   **Protein & Calcium:** In tiny quantities, cheese provides a small boost of these nutrients.

### 🔴 The Potential Risks
*   **Digestive Upset:** Lactose intolerance can lead to diarrhea, gas, and vomiting.
*   **Weight Gain:** Cheese is calorie-dense and high in fat, which can lead to obesity.
*   **Sodium Overload:** Many cheeses are high in salt, which can put pressure on a cat's kidneys.
*   **Toxic Additives:** Some specialty cheeses contain garlic or onion powder, which are **toxic** to cats.

---

## How to Safely Feed Cheese to Your Oriental Shorthair

If you want to give your cat a cheesy treat, follow these safety guidelines to keep their tummy happy:

### 1. Choose the Right Type
Opt for hard, aged cheeses. The aging process breaks down much of the lactose.
*   **Best Choices:** Small amounts of Cheddar, Parmesan, or Swiss.
*   **Avoid:** Soft cheeses (like Brie or Camembert) and processed "cheese products" with artificial additives.

### 2. Keep Portions Tiny
A "treat" should be no larger than the size of a **small die (about 1cm cube)**. Cheese should never exceed 10% of your cat's daily caloric intake.

### 3. Check the Ingredients
Ensure the cheese is plain. Avoid any cheese seasoned with:
*   Garlic
*   Onions
*   Chives
*   Excessive herbs or spices

### 4. Monitor for Reactions
The first time you offer cheese, give a tiny piece and monitor your cat for 24 hours. Look for signs of stomach upset or skin allergies.

**Pro Tip:** If your Oriental Shorthair loves the taste but gets an upset stomach, consider buying **lactose-free cheese** specifically designed for pets.
