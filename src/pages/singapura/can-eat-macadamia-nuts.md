# Can a Singapura Eat Macadamia Nuts?

**Verdict: ❌ NO**

## Why You Should Avoid Macadamia Nuts
While macadamia nuts are famously toxic to dogs, they are not recommended for cats—including the Singapura breed. Cats are **obligate carnivores**, meaning their bodies are designed to process animal proteins and fats, not the complex fats and oils found in macadamia nuts. 

Feeding these nuts to your Singapura can lead to severe digestive distress and other systemic health issues.

## Potential Risks to Your Singapura
Because Singapura cats are naturally small and lean, they are particularly sensitive to dietary indiscretions. The risks include:

*   **Pancreatitis:** The extremely high fat content in macadamia nuts can trigger inflammation of the pancreas, a painful and potentially life-threatening condition.
*   **Gastrointestinal Upset:** Your cat may experience vomiting, diarrhea, and abdominal pain.
*   **Lethargy:** In some cases, ingestion can lead to weakness or a lack of coordination.
*   **Choking Hazard:** Due to their size and shape, whole macadamia nuts pose a significant choking risk for a small cat.

## What to Do If Your Singapura Ate a Macadamia Nut
If your cat has managed to sneak a nut, do not panic, but take the following steps immediately:

1.  **Remove Access:** Ensure no more nuts are within reach.
2.  **Monitor Closely:** Watch for signs of vomiting, lethargy, or loss of appetite over the next 24–48 hours.
3.  **Call Your Vet:** Contact your veterinarian or a pet poison control center. Provide them with the estimated amount consumed and your cat's weight.
4.  **Avoid Home Remedies:** Do not attempt to induce vomiting unless specifically instructed to do so by a licensed professional.

### Safe Alternatives for Your Singapura
If you want to give your Singapura a special treat, stick to cat-safe options such as:
*   Small pieces of cooked chicken or turkey (unseasoned).
*   Commercial cat treats formulated for their specific nutritional needs.
*   Small bits of steamed fish (salmon or tuna).
