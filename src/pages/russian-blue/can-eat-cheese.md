# Can a Russian Blue Eat Cheese? 🧀

**Verdict: Yes, but only in moderation and as an occasional treat.**

While a small piece of cheese won't typically harm your Russian Blue, it is not a natural part of their feline diet. Most adult cats are lactose intolerant, meaning they lack the enzyme (lactase) needed to digest the sugars in dairy.

---

## Why the Caution?
Russian Blues, like all domestic cats, are **obligate carnivores**. Their bodies are designed to process animal proteins and fats, not dairy. While cheese has a lower lactose content than a bowl of milk, it can still cause digestive upset for many cats.

### ⚠️ Potential Risks
*   **Lactose Intolerance:** The most common issue. Symptoms include diarrhea, gas, and vomiting.
*   **High Sodium:** Many cheeses are loaded with salt, which can strain a cat's kidneys over time.
*   **Weight Gain:** Cheese is calorie-dense and high in fat, which can lead to obesity if fed frequently.
*   **Toxic Additives:** Some specialty cheeses contain garlic or onion powder, which are **toxic** to cats.

### ✅ Potential Benefits
To be honest, there are very few nutritional benefits to feeding cheese to a Russian Blue. While it provides a small amount of protein and calcium, these are already present in high-quality cat food in a more bioavailable form. For most cats, cheese is simply a "high-value" taste treat.

---

## How to Safely Feed Cheese to Your Russian Blue

If your Russian Blue loves cheese and doesn't show signs of digestive distress, follow these safety guidelines:

1.  **Start Small:** Offer a piece the size of a thumbnail to check for an adverse reaction.
2.  **Choose Wisely:** Opt for hard, aged cheeses like **Parmesan, Cheddar, or Swiss**. These generally have lower lactose levels than soft cheeses like Mozzarella or Brie.
3.  **Avoid "Flavor" Cheeses:** Never feed cheese that contains herbs, garlic, onions, or excessive spices.
4.  **Keep it a Rare Treat:** Cheese should make up less than 10% of your cat's daily caloric intake.

### What to do if your cat ate too much cheese?
If your Russian Blue accidentally gorged themselves on a cheese platter, monitor them for the next 24 hours. 
*   **Mild upset:** Provide plenty of fresh water to help flush their system.
*   **Severe symptoms:** If you notice persistent vomiting or severe diarrhea, contact your veterinarian immediately.
