# Can a Poodle Eat Celery?

**Verdict: ✅ YES**

Poodles can safely eat celery. It is a non-toxic vegetable that makes for a crunchy, low-calorie treat for dogs of all sizes.

---

### Why Celery is Safe for Poodles
Celery is primarily composed of water and fiber, making it a refreshing snack that doesn't add significant calories to your dog's diet. While Poodles are known for being intelligent and active, they can also be prone to weight gain if overfed; celery provides a satisfying "crunch" without the caloric burden of commercial treats.

### Potential Benefits & Risks

#### 🌟 The Benefits
*   **Low Calorie:** Ideal for Poodles needing to maintain a healthy weight.
*   **Hydration:** High water content helps keep your dog hydrated.
*   **Vitamin Boost:** Contains small amounts of Vitamin A, Vitamin C, and Potassium.
*   **Dental Health:** The fibrous texture can help gently clean teeth and freshen breath.

#### ⚠️ The Risks
*   **Choking Hazard:** The long, stringy fibers in celery can be difficult for some dogs to chew or swallow.
*   **Digestive Upset:** Introducing too much fiber too quickly can lead to gas or diarrhea.
*   **Sodium Content:** Never feed your Poodle celery that has been salted or seasoned.

---

### How to Safely Feed Celery to Your Poodle

To ensure your Poodle enjoys their snack without any risks, follow these preparation steps:

1.  **Wash Thoroughly:** Rinse the celery under cold water to remove any pesticides or dirt.
2.  **Cut into Small Pieces:** Do not feed long stalks. Dice the celery into **small, bite-sized cubes** to prevent choking and aid digestion.
3.  **Start Slow:** Give your Poodle one or two small pieces first to ensure they don't have a sensitive stomach.
4.  **Moderation is Key:** Celery should be a treat, not a meal replacement. It should make up no more than 10% of their daily caloric intake.

***Disclaimer:** While celery is generally safe, every dog is unique. If your Poodle has specific health conditions or kidney issues, consult your veterinarian before introducing new foods into their diet.*
