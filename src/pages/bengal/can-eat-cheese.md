# Can a Bengal Eat Cheese? The Quick Guide

**Verdict: YES (But only in small, occasional amounts)**

While cheese is not toxic to Bengal cats, it is not a natural part of their feline diet. Most cats are lactose intolerant, meaning they lack the enzyme (lactase) needed to digest the sugars found in dairy.

---

### Why the Caution?
Bengal cats are highly active and intelligent, but their digestive systems are designed for animal proteins and fats. While a tiny cube of cheese likely won't cause harm, giving too much can lead to gastrointestinal distress or long-term health issues.

### Potential Risks & Benefits

#### ⚠️ The Risks
*   **Lactose Intolerance:** The most common risk. Symptoms include diarrhea, gas, and vomiting.
*   **High Caloric Density:** Cheese is fat-heavy, which can lead to obesity if fed regularly.
*   **Sodium Content:** Many cheeses are high in salt, which can strain a cat's kidneys over time.
*   **Toxic Additives:** Some gourmet cheeses contain garlic or onion powder, which are **toxic** to cats.

#### ✅ The Benefits
*   **Palatability:** Most Bengals love the taste, making it a high-value reward for training.
*   **Protein/Calcium:** In microscopic amounts, cheese provides these nutrients, though they are better sourced from cat-specific foods.

---

### How to Safely Feed Cheese to Your Bengal

If you want to give your Bengal a cheesy treat, follow these safety guidelines:

1.  **Start Tiny:** Offer a piece the size of a pea to check for adverse reactions.
2.  **Choose Hard Cheeses:** Opt for aged cheeses like **Cheddar, Parmesan, or Swiss**. These have lower lactose levels than soft cheeses like Mozzarella or Brie.
3.  **Avoid Flavorings:** Never feed cheese containing herbs, garlic, onions, or high amounts of salt.
4.  **Moderation is Key:** Cheese should be a "once-in-a-while" treat, not a meal replacement. It should make up less than 10% of their daily caloric intake.

**Pro Tip:** If your Bengal has a sensitive stomach, skip the cheese entirely and opt for a freeze-dried chicken or salmon treat instead!
