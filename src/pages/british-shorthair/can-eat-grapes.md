# Can a British Shorthair Eat Grapes? 🍇

**Verdict: ❌ NO**

---

## Why You Should Never Feed Grapes to Your British Shorthair
Regardless of the breed, **grapes and raisins are highly toxic to cats.** While the exact toxin is still being studied, recent research points to **tartaric acid** as the primary culprit. 

Because British Shorthairs are obligate carnivores, their bodies are not equipped to process the compounds found in grapes. Even a single grape or raisin can trigger a severe adverse reaction in some cats.

---

## The Risks: What Happens if They Eat Grapes?
Feeding your cat grapes isn't just a "stomach ache" risk—it is a life-threatening emergency. The primary danger is **acute kidney failure**.

### Potential Symptoms to Watch For:
*   **Vomiting and Diarrhea:** Often the first sign of toxicity.
*   **Lethargy:** Unusual tiredness or lack of energy.
*   **Loss of Appetite:** Refusing their favorite wet food or treats.
*   **Dehydration:** Increased thirst or, conversely, a complete stop in urine production (a sign of kidney failure).
*   **Disorientation:** Acting confused or unsteady on their feet.

---

## What to Do If Your Cat Ate a Grape
If your British Shorthair has accidentally ingested a grape or raisin, **time is of the essence.** Do not wait for symptoms to appear, as kidney damage can happen rapidly.

### Immediate Action Steps:
1.  **Call Your Veterinarian Immediately:** Contact your vet or the nearest emergency animal clinic.
2.  **Provide Details:** Tell the vet how many grapes were eaten, the approximate time of ingestion, and your cat's current weight.
3.  **Do Not Induce Vomiting:** Never attempt to make your cat vomit at home unless explicitly instructed to do so by a professional, as this can cause aspiration pneumonia.
4.  **Seek Diagnostic Tests:** Your vet will likely perform blood tests to check kidney function and may administer IV fluids to flush the toxins from the system.

### Safe Alternatives for Your British Shorthair
If you want to give your cat a treat, stick to cat-safe options:
*   **Freeze-dried chicken or salmon**
*   **Small pieces of cooked pumpkin** (plain, no additives)
*   **Commercial cat treats** specifically formulated for their nutritional needs.
