# Can a Great Dane Eat Grapes? The Essential Safety Guide

**Verdict: ❌ NO**

**Absolutely not. You should never feed grapes or raisins to a Great Dane, regardless of their size.**

---

## Why Grapes are Dangerous for Great Danes
While Great Danes are giants among dogs, their internal organs are just as susceptible to toxins as a small breed. Grapes and raisins contain **tartaric acid**, which is highly toxic to dogs. 

Unlike some toxins that are dose-dependent based on weight, grapes can trigger **acute kidney failure** in any dog. Even a single grape can be fatal for some, meaning your dog's large size does not provide a "safety buffer."

## The Risks: What Happens if They Eat Grapes?
If a Great Dane consumes grapes or raisins, they are at risk for sudden renal failure. Keep a close eye out for these warning signs:

*   **Gastrointestinal Distress:** Vomiting and diarrhea within a few hours.
*   **Lethargy:** Unusual tiredness or weakness.
*   **Loss of Appetite:** Refusing their usual meals.
*   **Dehydration:** Increased thirst or, conversely, a complete stop in urination (a sign of kidney failure).

## What to Do if Your Great Dane Ate Grapes
If your dog has accidentally ingested grapes or raisins, **treat this as a medical emergency.** Do not wait for symptoms to appear, as kidney damage can happen rapidly.

1.  **Call Your Veterinarian Immediately:** Contact your vet or the nearest emergency animal hospital.
2.  **Contact Pet Poison Control:** You can call the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at (888) 420-9000.
3.  **Provide Details:** Be ready to tell the vet how many grapes were eaten and how long ago the incident occurred.
4.  **Do Not Induce Vomiting** unless specifically instructed to do so by a veterinary professional.

## Safe, Dog-Friendly Alternatives
If you want to give your Great Dane a fruity treat, stick to these safe, kidney-friendly options:
*   **Blueberries** (Rich in antioxidants)
*   **Apple Slices** (Seedless and core-free)
*   **Watermelon** (Seedless and rind-free)
*   **Banana** (In moderation)
