# Can Balinese Cats Eat Tomatoes? Safe Treats or Hidden Danger?

**Verdict: YES (But only the ripe fruit)**

While your elegant Balinese cat can safely nibble on a piece of **ripe, red tomato**, you must be extremely cautious. Not all parts of the tomato plant are safe for felines.

---

## Why the Caution?
The primary concern is a compound called **solanine**. Solanine is a natural toxin found in the green parts of the tomato plant, including the stems, leaves, and unripened green tomatoes. 

While a fully ripe red tomato contains negligible amounts of solanine, the green portions can cause toxicity in cats, leading to gastrointestinal distress or worse.

---

## Potential Benefits & Risks

### 🟢 The Benefits (In Moderation)
Ripe tomatoes contain antioxidants like **lycopene** and vitamins A and C. While Balinese cats are obligate carnivores and get their primary nutrients from meat, a tiny piece of tomato is a harmless, low-calorie treat.

### 🔴 The Risks
*   **Solanine Poisoning:** Ingesting green tomatoes or plant foliage can lead to vomiting, diarrhea, and lethargy.
*   **Digestive Upset:** Some cats have sensitive stomachs; introducing new vegetables can cause mild nausea.
*   **Added Ingredients:** Many "tomato products" (ketchup, pasta sauce) contain garlic or onion powder, both of which are **highly toxic** to cats.

---

## How to Safely Feed Tomatoes to Your Balinese

If you want to share a snack with your cat, follow these strict guidelines:

1.  **Ripe Only:** Only feed the fleshy, red part of the tomato.
2.  **Remove All Greenery:** Ensure there are no stems or leaves attached to the piece you give them.
3.  **Plain and Raw:** Never feed your cat seasoned tomatoes. No salt, pepper, oil, or sugar.
4.  **Small Portions:** Treat it as a "once-in-a-while" snack, not a meal replacement. A small cube (half an inch) is plenty.

### ⚠️ What to do if your cat eats a green tomato:
If your Balinese cat consumes a green tomato or a tomato plant leaf, contact your veterinarian immediately. Watch for these signs of solanine poisoning:
*   Excessive drooling
*   Vomiting
*   Lack of coordination (ataxia)
*   Extreme lethargy
