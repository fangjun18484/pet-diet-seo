# Can a Cairn Terrier Eat Grapes?

**Verdict: ❌ NO**

## Why Grapes Are Dangerous for Cairn Terriers
Regardless of the breed, **grapes and raisins are highly toxic to dogs.** While some dogs may seem fine after eating one, others can experience severe reactions. In Cairn Terriers—and all canines—grapes can cause **acute kidney failure**, which can be fatal if not treated immediately.

The exact toxin responsible for this reaction is not fully understood, but recent research suggests that **tartaric acid** in grapes is the likely culprit, triggering sudden renal failure.

## The Risks of Feeding Grapes to Your Dog
There are **no nutritional benefits** to feeding grapes to a Cairn Terrier; the risks far outweigh any perceived "healthy snack" value. 

**Potential symptoms of grape poisoning include:**
*   **Vomiting and Diarrhea:** Often occurring shortly after ingestion.
*   **Lethargy:** Unusual tiredness or weakness.
*   **Loss of Appetite:** Refusing food or water.
*   **Dehydration:** Dry gums and skin.
*   **Decreased Urination:** A critical sign that the kidneys are failing.

## What to Do If Your Cairn Terrier Ate Grapes
If your dog has ingested a grape or raisin, **treat it as a medical emergency.** Do not wait for symptoms to appear, as kidney damage can happen quickly.

1.  **Call Your Veterinarian Immediately:** Contact your local vet or an emergency animal clinic right away.
2.  **Contact Pet Poison Control:** You can call the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center or a similar service for guidance.
3.  **Provide Details:** Be ready to tell the vet how many grapes were eaten, the approximate time of ingestion, and the weight of your dog.
4.  **Do Not Induce Vomiting Unless Instructed:** Only induce vomiting if your veterinarian explicitly tells you to do so over the phone.

### Safe Alternatives for Your Cairn Terrier
If you want to give your Cairn Terrier a fruity treat, try these dog-safe options instead:
*   **Blueberries** (Rich in antioxidants)
*   **Apple slices** (Without seeds or core)
*   **Watermelon** (Seedless and rind-free)
*   **Banana** (In moderation)
