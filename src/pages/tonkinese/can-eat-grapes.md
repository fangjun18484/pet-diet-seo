# Can a Tonkinese Eat Grapes? 🍇

**Verdict: ❌ NO**

---

## Why You Should Never Feed Grapes to Your Tonkinese
Regardless of the breed, **grapes and raisins are highly toxic to all cats**, including the Tonkinese. While the exact toxin has not been fully identified, scientific evidence shows that these fruits can trigger sudden and severe **acute kidney failure** in feline companions.

Because Tonkinese cats are known for being curious and active, it is vital to keep these fruits stored in secure containers where they cannot be reached.

---

## The Risks: What Happens if They Eat Grapes?
There are no nutritional benefits to feeding grapes to a cat; only severe health risks. Even a single grape or raisin can be dangerous depending on the cat's size and sensitivity.

**Potential symptoms of grape poisoning include:**
*   **Vomiting and Diarrhea:** Often the first sign of distress.
*   **Lethargy:** Unusual tiredness or lack of energy.
*   **Loss of Appetite:** Refusing their favorite treats or meals.
*   **Dehydration:** Increased thirst or, conversely, a decrease in urine output (a sign of kidney failure).
*   **Neurological Issues:** In advanced stages, disorientation or seizures.

---

## What to Do if Your Tonkinese Ate a Grape
If you suspect your cat has ingested a grape or raisin, **this is a veterinary emergency.** Do not wait for symptoms to appear, as kidney damage can happen quickly.

### Immediate Action Plan:
1.  **Call Your Vet Immediately:** Contact your primary veterinarian or the nearest emergency animal clinic.
2.  **Prevent Further Access:** Remove any remaining grapes from the area.
3.  **Provide Information:** Be ready to tell the vet **how many** grapes were eaten and **how long ago** the incident occurred.
4.  **Do Not Induce Vomiting:** Never attempt to make your cat vomit unless specifically instructed to do so by a licensed professional.

### Safe Alternatives for Your Tonkinese
If you want to give your Tonkinese a healthy, fruit-based treat, try these cat-safe options in moderation:
*   **Blueberries** (in small quantities)
*   **Cranberries**
*   **Pumpkin** (plain, unsweetened)
