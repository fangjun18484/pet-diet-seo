# Can American Shorthairs Eat Grapes? 🍇

**Verdict: ❌ NO**

American Shorthairs, like all cat breeds, **should never eat grapes or raisins.** Grapes are highly toxic to cats and can lead to severe health complications.

---

## Why Are Grapes Dangerous for Cats?
While the exact toxin in grapes was long debated, recent research suggests that **tartaric acid** is the primary culprit. Cats' bodies cannot process this compound, which can lead to sudden and acute **kidney failure**. 

Even a single grape or raisin can be toxic depending on the size of the cat and the specific grape variety, making it a high-risk food.

---

## Potential Risks & Symptoms
Feeding a grape to your American Shorthair is not a minor mistake; it is a medical emergency. The risks include:

*   **Acute Renal Failure:** The most severe risk, where the kidneys stop filtering waste from the blood.
*   **Gastrointestinal Distress:** Severe vomiting and diarrhea.
*   **Lethargy:** Sudden loss of energy or depression.
*   **Loss of Appetite:** Refusing food and water.
*   **Dehydration:** Due to the inability of the kidneys to regulate fluids.

---

## What to Do If Your Cat Ate a Grape
If your American Shorthair has ingested a grape or raisin, **time is of the essence.** Do not wait for symptoms to appear, as kidney damage can happen quickly.

### Immediate Action Steps:
1.  **Call Your Veterinarian Immediately:** Contact your local clinic or an emergency pet hospital.
2.  **Contact Pet Poison Control:** If your vet is unavailable, call a dedicated animal poison hotline.
3.  **Gather Information:** Try to determine exactly how many grapes were eaten and how long ago it happened.
4.  **Do Not Induce Vomiting:** Never try to make your cat vomit unless specifically instructed to do so by a licensed veterinarian.

### Safe Alternatives for Your Cat
If you want to give your American Shorthair a healthy treat, skip the fruit and try these vet-approved options:
*   **Small pieces of cooked chicken** (plain, no seasoning).
*   **A tiny piece of steamed salmon.**
*   **Commercial cat treats** formulated for their specific nutritional needs.
