# Can Border Collies Eat Avocado?

**Verdict: YES (In Moderation)**

While avocado is not toxic to dogs in the same way grapes or chocolate are, it should be given sparingly and with caution.

---

### Why the Caution?
The primary concern with avocados is a toxin called **persin**. While persin is found in the leaves, bark, and pit of the avocado tree, the levels in the actual fruit flesh are generally low enough that most dogs can tolerate them. 

However, Border Collies—known for their high energy and sensitive systems—can still be prone to digestive upset if they consume too much.

---

### Potential Benefits vs. Risks

#### ✅ The Benefits (Small Amounts)
*   **Healthy Fats:** Rich in monounsaturated fats which can support a shiny coat and healthy skin.
*   **Vitamins:** Contains potassium and Vitamin E.
*   **Fiber:** Can aid in digestion when given in tiny quantities.

#### ❌ The Risks (The Danger Zones)
*   **Pancreatitis:** Avocados are very high in fat. For some dogs, this can trigger pancreatitis (inflammation of the pancreas), which is a serious medical condition.
*   **Choking Hazard:** The large avocado pit is a major choking risk and can cause an intestinal blockage if swallowed.
*   **GI Upset:** Too much avocado can lead to vomiting or diarrhea.

---

### How to Safely Feed Avocado to Your Border Collie

If you want to share a piece of avocado with your dog, follow these strict safety guidelines:

1.  **Flesh Only:** Never let your dog chew on the skin or the pit.
2.  **Small Portions:** Treat it as a rare snack, not a meal. A small cube (roughly the size of a dice) is plenty.
3.  **Plain Only:** Ensure the avocado is plain. **Avoid** guacamole or store-bought dips, as these often contain garlic and onion powder, which are **toxic** to dogs.
4.  **Watch for Reactions:** The first time you feed it, give a tiny piece and monitor for any signs of stomach distress.

### What to do if your dog eats the pit?
If your Border Collie swallows an avocado pit, **contact your veterinarian immediately**. Because of the size and hardness of the pit, it is highly likely to cause a gastrointestinal obstruction that may require surgical removal.
