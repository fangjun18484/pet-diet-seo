# Can Border Collies Eat Cheese? The Quick Guide for Dog Owners

**Verdict: YES (In moderation)**

While Border Collies can safely eat cheese, it should be treated as an occasional treat rather than a staple of their diet. Not all cheeses are created equal, and some can cause digestive upset in your high-energy pup.

---

## Why Cheese is Generally Safe (But Risky)
Most dogs can tolerate small amounts of cheese. However, many dogs are **lactose intolerant**. Because dogs lack the enzyme (lactase) needed to break down the sugars in dairy, too much cheese can lead to gastrointestinal distress.

For a Border Collie—a breed known for its high intelligence and activity levels—cheese can be a great high-value reward for training, provided it doesn't lead to weight gain.

---

## Potential Benefits & Risks

### ✅ The Benefits
*   **Protein & Calcium:** Cheese provides a quick boost of protein and essential minerals for bone health.
*   **High-Value Motivation:** Because of the strong scent and taste, cheese is an excellent motivator for teaching complex tricks to Border Collies.

### ⚠️ The Risks
*   **Lactose Intolerance:** Can cause gas, bloating, and diarrhea.
*   **High Caloric Density:** Excess cheese can lead to obesity, which puts pressure on a Border Collie's joints.
*   **Sodium Content:** High salt levels in some cheeses can be harmful to a dog's kidneys over time.
*   **Toxic Additives:** Some gourmet cheeses contain garlic or onion powder, which are **toxic** to dogs.

---

## How to Safely Feed Cheese to Your Border Collie

If you want to share a snack with your dog, follow these nutritionist-approved guidelines:

### 1. Choose the Right Type
Stick to plain, low-sodium, and lower-fat options:
*   **Best Choices:** Cottage cheese, Mozzarella, or mild Cheddar.
*   **Avoid:** Blue cheese, Brie, or any cheese flavored with herbs, garlic, or onions.

### 2. Portion Control
Keep it to a "treat" status. Cheese should make up **no more than 10%** of your dog's daily caloric intake. A few small cubes are plenty.

### 3. Start Small
If your Border Collie has never had dairy, give them one tiny piece and monitor them for 24 hours for any signs of an upset stomach.

### 4. Check the Ingredients
Always read the label. Ensure there are no artificial sweeteners (like Xylitol) or preservatives that could be harmful.

**Pro Tip:** If your Border Collie has a sensitive stomach, consider **lactose-free cheese** or freeze-dried liver as a healthier alternative for training!
