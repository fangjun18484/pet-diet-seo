# Can a Border Collie Eat Chocolate?

**Verdict: ❌ NO**

Border Collies, like all dogs, should **never** eat chocolate. Regardless of the breed's high intelligence or energy levels, chocolate contains compounds that are toxic to canine physiology.

---

### Why is Chocolate Dangerous for Border Collies?
The primary culprit is a methylxanthine called **theobromine**, along with smaller amounts of **caffeine**. 

While humans can easily metabolize these stimulants, dogs process them much more slowly. This allows the chemicals to build up to toxic levels in their system, overstimulating the central nervous system and the heart.

---

### The Risks: What Happens if They Eat It?
There are no benefits to feeding a Border Collie chocolate; only significant health risks. The severity of the toxicity depends on the type of chocolate and the size of the dog.

#### ⚠️ Potential Symptoms of Chocolate Toxicity:
*   **Hyperactivity:** Extreme restlessness or pacing (which can look like "zoomies" but is actually distress).
*   **Digestive Distress:** Vomiting and diarrhea.
*   **Cardiovascular Issues:** Increased heart rate, arrhythmias, or hypertension.
*   **Neurological Crisis:** Muscle tremors, seizures, and in severe cases, coma.

#### 🍫 Toxicity Scale by Chocolate Type:
*   **White Chocolate:** Low risk (high fat, but low theobromine).
*   **Milk Chocolate:** Moderate risk.
*   **Dark Chocolate/Cocoa Powder:** **High risk** (contains the highest concentration of theobromine).

---

### What to Do If Your Border Collie Ate Chocolate
If your dog has ingested chocolate, **do not wait for symptoms to appear.** Act quickly to minimize absorption.

1.  **Identify the Amount:** Determine exactly what kind of chocolate was eaten and approximately how much.
2.  **Call Your Vet Immediately:** Contact your veterinarian or an emergency animal clinic.
3.  **Contact Pet Poison Control:** If a vet isn't available, call the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center.
4.  **Do Not Induce Vomiting Unless Instructed:** Only induce vomiting if specifically told to do so by a medical professional, as doing it incorrectly can cause aspiration.

**Pro Tip:** To keep your Border Collie safe, store all sweets in high cabinets or locked containers. If you want to give your pup a treat, stick to dog-safe alternatives like **blueberries, sliced carrots, or plain cooked pumpkin.**
