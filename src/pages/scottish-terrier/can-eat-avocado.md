# Can a Scottish Terrier Eat Avocado?

**Verdict: ❌ NO (Avoid feeding it to them)**

While a small lick of avocado flesh may not be immediately fatal for most dogs, it is **not recommended** for Scottish Terriers. The potential risks far outweigh any nutritional benefits.

---

## Why You Should Avoid Avocado
The primary concern with avocados is a fungicidal toxin called **persin**. While dogs are more resistant to persin than animals like parrots or horses, it can still cause gastrointestinal distress in some pups.

More importantly, avocados are incredibly dense in fats. While "healthy fats" are good for humans, the high fat content in avocados can trigger severe health issues in dogs, particularly smaller breeds like the Scottish Terrier.

---

## Potential Risks to Your Scottie

### 1. Pancreatitis
Scottish Terriers can be sensitive to high-fat diets. Consuming avocado can lead to **pancreatitis**—an inflammation of the pancreas that causes severe abdominal pain, vomiting, and diarrhea. This is a medical emergency.

### 2. Choking and Intestinal Blockage
The avocado pit is a massive hazard. Due to its size and shape, if a Scottie swallows the pit, it can easily become lodged in the throat or cause a **life-threatening intestinal blockage** that requires surgical removal.

### 3. Digestive Upset
Even without pancreatitis, the richness of the fruit often leads to loose stools or nausea in dogs.

---

## What to Do If Your Scottish Terrier Ate Avocado

If your dog managed to sneak a piece of avocado, don't panic, but stay vigilant.

### If they ate a small amount of flesh:
* **Monitor closely:** Watch for vomiting, diarrhea, or lethargy.
* **Limit other fats:** Keep their next few meals lean to avoid stressing the pancreas.
* **Provide plenty of water:** Ensure they stay hydrated.

### If they swallowed the PIT or a large amount:
**Contact your veterinarian or an animal poison control center immediately.** 

**Watch for these "Red Flag" symptoms:**
* Repeated vomiting.
* Loss of appetite.
* Straining to defecate or lack of bowel movements.
* A hard, painful abdomen.

### Safe Alternatives for Your Scottie
If you want to give your Scottish Terrier a healthy, creamy treat, try these dog-safe options instead:
* **Blueberries** (Antioxidant-rich)
* **Apple slices** (Seedless and skinless)
* **Plain canned pumpkin** (Great for digestion)
* **Carrot sticks** (Low calorie and crunchy)
