# Can a Scottish Terrier Eat Peanut Butter?

**Verdict: YES (But only if it is Xylitol-free!)**

While Scottish Terriers can safely enjoy peanut butter, it should be treated as an occasional snack rather than a staple of their diet. Not all peanut butters are created equal, and some ingredients can be life-threatening to your dog.

---

### Why Peanut Butter is Generally Safe
Peanut butter is packed with protein, healthy fats, and vitamins (like Vitamin E and Niacin). For a spirited breed like the Scottish Terrier, it can be a great high-value reward for training or a tasty filler for puzzle toys to keep them mentally stimulated.

---

### The Benefits vs. The Risks

#### 🌟 The Benefits
*   **Protein Boost:** Supports muscle maintenance for active terriers.
*   **Healthy Fats:** Promotes a shiny coat and healthy skin.
*   **Mental Stimulation:** Slowing down their eating by putting peanut butter in a Kong toy reduces boredom.

#### ⚠️ The Risks
*   **Xylitol Toxicity:** This is the biggest danger. Xylitol is an artificial sweetener found in some "low sugar" or "diet" peanut butters. It is **highly toxic** to dogs and can cause liver failure or death.
*   **Weight Gain:** Scotties are prone to obesity if overfed. Peanut butter is calorie-dense.
*   **Pancreatitis:** The high fat content can trigger pancreatitis in sensitive dogs.
*   **Allergies:** Some dogs are allergic to peanuts, which can cause itching or digestive upset.

---

### How to Safely Serve Peanut Butter to Your Scottie

To ensure your Scottish Terrier enjoys this treat safely, follow these golden rules:

1.  **Read the Ingredients:** Only buy peanut butter where the ingredients are simply **peanuts and salt**. Avoid any mention of Xylitol, sugar, or palm oil.
2.  **Moderation is Key:** Limit peanut butter to a small spoonful or a teaspoon a few times a week. It should not exceed 10% of their daily caloric intake.
3.  **Watch the Salt:** Choose "unsalted" or "low sodium" varieties to protect your dog's kidney health.
4.  **Introduce Slowly:** Give a tiny amount first to ensure your dog doesn't have an allergic reaction.

**Pro Tip:** If you want a healthier alternative, try **pumpkin puree** (plain) or **plain Greek yogurt** for a lower-calorie treat!
