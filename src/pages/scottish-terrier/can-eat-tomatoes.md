# Can a Scottish Terrier Eat Tomatoes?

**Verdict: YES (But only if they are ripe and red)**

While a piece of a ripe tomato is generally safe for your Scottish Terrier, you must be extremely cautious about which parts of the plant your dog consumes.

## Why the Caution?
The primary concern with tomatoes is a substance called **solanine**. Solanine is a toxic alkaloid found in the nightshade family of plants. 

While the flesh of a fully ripe, red tomato contains negligible amounts of solanine, the **green parts of the plant**—including the stems, leaves, and unripe green tomatoes—contain high concentrations. If ingested, solanine can cause toxicity in dogs.

## Potential Benefits & Risks

### The Benefits (Ripe Tomatoes Only)
When fed in moderation, ripe tomatoes provide:
*   **Lycopene:** A powerful antioxidant that supports heart health.
*   **Potassium:** Essential for nerve and muscle function.
*   **Vitamin C:** Supports the immune system.

### The Risks (Green Parts & Unripe Fruit)
If your Scottie eats green tomatoes or the tomato vine, they may experience **solanine poisoning**. Symptoms include:
*   Lethargy or depression.
*   Gastrointestinal distress (vomiting and diarrhea).
*   Muscle weakness or lack of coordination.
*   Increased heart rate.

## How to Safely Feed Tomatoes to Your Scottish Terrier

If you want to give your Scottie a taste of tomato, follow these strict guidelines:

1.  **Check the Color:** Only feed tomatoes that are deep red and fully ripe.
2.  **Remove the Greenery:** Carefully strip away all leaves, stems, and any green-tinted areas of the fruit.
3.  **Avoid Seasonings:** Never feed your dog tomatoes prepared for humans (e.g., those with salt, garlic, or onion powder), as these ingredients can be toxic.
4.  **Moderation is Key:** Tomatoes should be an occasional treat, not a meal replacement. A small slice or two is plenty.

### ⚠️ What to do if your dog ate a green tomato:
If your Scottish Terrier has chewed on a tomato plant or eaten an unripe green tomato, **contact your veterinarian or a pet poison control center immediately.** Early intervention is key to managing solanine toxicity.
