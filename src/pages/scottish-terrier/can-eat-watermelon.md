# Can a Scottish Terrier Eat Watermelon?

**Verdict: ✅ YES**

Scottish Terriers can safely enjoy watermelon as an occasional treat. It is a refreshing, low-calorie snack that is generally well-tolerated by most dogs, including "Scotties."

---

### Why is Watermelon Safe for Scottish Terriers?
Watermelon is primarily composed of water (about 92%), making it an excellent hydrating snack, especially during hot summer months. It contains no toxic compounds that would harm a dog's system, provided it is served correctly.

### The Benefits & Risks

#### 🌟 The Benefits
*   **Hydration:** Great for keeping your dog hydrated.
*   **Essential Nutrients:** Packed with vitamins A, B6, and C, as well as potassium.
*   **Low Calorie:** A great alternative to processed dog treats for weight management.

#### ⚠️ The Risks
*   **The Rind:** The hard outer shell is difficult to digest and can cause gastrointestinal blockage.
*   **The Seeds:** While not toxic, seeds can cause intestinal obstructions, particularly in smaller breeds like the Scottish Terrier.
*   **Sugar Content:** Watermelon contains natural sugars. While safe in moderation, too much can lead to weight gain or be dangerous for dogs with diabetes.

---

### How to Safely Serve Watermelon to Your Scottie

To ensure your Scottish Terrier enjoys their treat without any health scares, follow these preparation steps:

1.  **Remove the Rind:** Cut away all of the green skin and the white pith. Give them only the red, fleshy part.
2.  **De-seed the Fruit:** Carefully remove all black and white seeds before serving.
3.  **Cut into Bite-Sized Pieces:** Scottish Terriers have smaller mouths; small cubes prevent choking.
4.  **Moderation is Key:** Serve watermelon as a treat, not a meal replacement. It should make up no more than 10% of their daily caloric intake.

**Pro Tip:** Freeze watermelon chunks for a cooling, long-lasting treat during the summer!

***Disclaimer:** Always introduce new foods slowly. If your dog has a pre-existing condition like diabetes, consult your veterinarian before adding fruit to their diet.*
