# Can a Scottish Terrier Eat Cheese?

**Verdict: YES (In Moderation)**

While cheese is not toxic to dogs, it should be treated as an occasional treat rather than a dietary staple. For a Scottish Terrier, moderation is key to avoiding digestive upset and weight gain.

## Why the Caution?
Most dogs are lactose intolerant to some degree. While cheese has less lactose than milk, it is still rich in fats and sodium. Scottish Terriers, in particular, can be prone to skin allergies and obesity, meaning high-fat treats can quickly lead to health complications if overfed.

## Potential Benefits & Risks

### The Benefits (Small Amounts)
*   **Protein & Calcium:** Cheese provides a quick boost of these essential nutrients.
*   **High-Value Reward:** Because of the strong scent and taste, cheese is an excellent tool for training stubborn Scotties.

### The Risks (Too Much)
*   **Digestive Distress:** Lactose intolerance can cause gas, bloating, and diarrhea.
*   **Pancreatitis:** The high fat content in some cheeses can trigger pancreatitis, a serious inflammation of the pancreas.
*   **Weight Gain:** Cheese is calorie-dense; excessive feeding can lead to obesity.
*   **Sodium Overload:** High salt content can put pressure on your dog's kidneys.

## How to Safely Feed Cheese to Your Scottie

If you want to share a piece of cheese with your Scottish Terrier, follow these nutritionist-approved guidelines:

### 1. Choose the Right Type
Avoid processed "cheese products" or heavily salted varieties. Instead, opt for:
*   **Low-fat Cottage Cheese:** Great for digestion.
*   **Mozzarella:** Generally lower in lactose and salt.
*   **Plain Cheddar:** Acceptable in very tiny amounts.

### 2. Avoid Toxic Additives
**Never** feed your dog cheese that contains:
*   **Garlic or Onion powder** (toxic to dogs).
*   **Blue cheese or Gorgonzola** (can contain molds that are harmful).
*   **Xylitol** (an artificial sweetener sometimes found in low-calorie cheeses).

### 3. Portion Control
Keep portions tiny. A "treat" for a Scottish Terrier should be no larger than a small cube (about 1/2 inch).

### 4. Monitor the Reaction
The first time you feed your dog cheese, give them a tiny piece and monitor them for 24 hours for any signs of itchy skin, stomach upset, or lethargy.

***Disclaimer:** Always consult with your veterinarian before introducing new human foods into your pet's diet, especially if your dog has pre-existing kidney issues or allergies.*
