# Can a Scottish Terrier Eat Blueberries?

**Verdict: ✅ YES**

Scottish Terriers can safely enjoy blueberries. These tiny "superfoods" are non-toxic to dogs and provide a nutrient-dense snack that fits well into a balanced diet.

---

## Why Blueberries Are Safe for Scotties
Blueberries are naturally low in calories and free from the toxins found in other fruits (like grapes or raisins). For a spirited breed like the Scottish Terrier, blueberries offer a healthy alternative to processed store-bought treats.

## Potential Benefits & Risks

### 🌟 The Benefits
*   **Antioxidant Powerhouse:** Rich in anthocyanins, which help combat free radicals and support overall cellular health.
*   **Immune Support:** Packed with Vitamin C and K to keep your dog's immune system robust.
*   **Digestive Health:** They provide a small amount of dietary fiber, aiding in smooth digestion.
*   **Low Calorie:** Great for maintaining a healthy weight in smaller terriers.

### ⚠️ The Risks
*   **Sugar Content:** While natural, blueberries contain sugar. Overfeeding can lead to weight gain or spike blood glucose levels in dogs with diabetes.
*   **Digestive Upset:** Introducing too many berries too quickly may cause mild gastrointestinal distress or loose stools.
*   **Choking Hazard:** While rare, very small dogs may occasionally choke on a berry if eaten too rapidly.

---

## How to Safely Feed Blueberries to Your Scottish Terrier

To ensure your Scottie gets the most benefit without the risk, follow these preparation guidelines:

1.  **Wash Thoroughly:** Always rinse blueberries under cold water to remove any pesticide residues or dirt.
2.  **Moderation is Key:** Follow the **"10% Rule"**—treats should never make up more than 10% of your dog's daily caloric intake. For a Scottish Terrier, a handful (5–10 berries) is usually plenty.
3.  **Fresh or Frozen:** You can serve them fresh or frozen. Frozen blueberries make a great, cooling treat during the summer months.
4.  **Avoid Additives:** **Never** feed your dog blueberries from a pie, jam, or dried versions containing added sugars or artificial sweeteners (like Xylitol), which are toxic.

**Pro Tip:** If it is your dog's first time trying blueberries, offer just one or two and monitor them for 24 hours to ensure they don't have any individual allergic reactions.
