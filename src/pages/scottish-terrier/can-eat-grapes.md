# Can Scottish Terriers Eat Grapes? The Essential Safety Guide

**Verdict: ❌ NO**

Scottish Terriers, like all dog breeds, **cannot** eat grapes. Grapes and raisins are highly toxic to dogs and can lead to severe health complications.

---

### Why Grapes Are Dangerous for Scottish Terriers
While some foods are mildly irritating to dogs, grapes and raisins are fundamentally toxic. Recent research suggests that **tartaric acid** found in grapes is the likely cause of toxicity. 

Unlike some toxins that affect dogs based on size, grape toxicity can be unpredictable. Even a single grape or raisin can trigger **acute kidney failure** in some dogs, regardless of the breed's size or overall health.

---

### Potential Risks & Symptoms
Feeding a Scottish Terrier grapes puts them at risk of sudden renal collapse. If your dog has ingested grapes, watch for these warning signs:

*   **Vomiting and Diarrhea:** Often the first sign of distress.
*   **Lethargy:** Unusual tiredness or lack of energy.
*   **Loss of Appetite:** Refusing their favorite treats or meals.
*   **Dehydration:** Increased thirst or, conversely, an inability to urinate (a sign of kidney failure).
*   **Muscle Weakness:** Difficulty standing or walking.

---

### What to Do If Your Scottish Terrier Ate Grapes
If you suspect your dog has eaten a grape or raisin, **this is a veterinary emergency.** Do not wait for symptoms to appear, as kidney damage can happen rapidly.

1.  **Call Your Vet Immediately:** Contact your primary veterinarian or the nearest emergency animal clinic.
2.  **Provide Details:** Tell the vet how many grapes were eaten, the variety, and approximately how long ago it happened.
3.  **Do Not Induce Vomiting Alone:** Never give your dog hydrogen peroxide or attempt to make them vomit unless specifically instructed to do so by a licensed veterinarian.
4.  **Seek Immediate Treatment:** Your vet may administer activated charcoal or IV fluids to help flush the toxins from the system and protect the kidneys.

---

### Safe, Healthy Alternatives
Want to give your Scottie a fruity treat? Try these dog-safe options instead:

*   **Blueberries:** Packed with antioxidants and similar in size to grapes.
*   **Apple Slices:** Great for crunch (ensure the core and seeds are removed).
*   **Watermelon:** Hydrating and sweet (remove the seeds and rind).
*   **Banana:** A great source of potassium in moderation.
