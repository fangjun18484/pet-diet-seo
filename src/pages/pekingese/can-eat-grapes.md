# Can a Pekingese Eat Grapes? The Essential Safety Guide

**Verdict: ❌ NO**

**Pekingese dogs cannot eat grapes.** Grapes and raisins are highly toxic to dogs, regardless of the breed. Feeding a grape to your Pekingese can lead to severe health complications and is considered a medical emergency.

---

## Why Are Grapes Dangerous for Pekingese?
Unlike some foods that cause mild stomach upset, grapes (and raisins) contain compounds that are **nephrotoxic**, meaning they are poisonous to the kidneys. 

While the exact toxin was long debated, recent research suggests that **tartaric acid** in grapes is the primary culprit. Because Pekingese are small dogs, their bodies are more susceptible to toxins; even a single grape or a small raisin can trigger a toxic reaction.

---

## Potential Risks & Symptoms
There are **no benefits** to feeding grapes to a dog. The risks are severe and can be fatal.

### Immediate Warning Signs:
*   **Vomiting and Diarrhea:** Often occurring within a few hours of ingestion.
*   **Lethargy:** Unusual tiredness or weakness.
*   **Loss of Appetite:** Refusing their favorite treats or meals.
*   **Dehydration:** Increased thirst or, conversely, a complete stop in urination.

### Long-Term Danger:
*   **Acute Kidney Failure:** The most critical risk. If the kidneys shut down, the body cannot filter toxins from the blood, which can lead to death if not treated immediately.

---

## What to Do If Your Pekingese Ate a Grape
If your Pekingese has ingested a grape or raisin, **do not wait for symptoms to appear.** Once symptoms start, kidney damage may already be advanced.

### Immediate Action Plan:
1.  **Call Your Veterinarian Immediately:** Contact your vet or the nearest emergency animal clinic.
2.  **Provide Details:** Tell the vet exactly how many grapes were eaten and how long ago it happened.
3.  **Do Not Induce Vomiting Unless Instructed:** Only attempt to make your dog vomit if your vet specifically tells you to do so over the phone.
4.  **Seek Medical Intervention:** Your vet may administer activated charcoal to bind the toxins or provide IV fluids to protect the kidneys.

### Safe Alternatives for Your Pekingese
If you want to give your Pekingese a fruity treat, try these **dog-safe** options instead:
*   **Blueberries** (Rich in antioxidants)
*   **Apple Slices** (Seedless and skinless)
*   **Watermelon** (Seedless and rind-free)
*   **Banana** (In moderation)
