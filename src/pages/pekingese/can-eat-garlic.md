# Can a Pekingese Eat Garlic?

**Verdict: ❌ NO**

---

## Why You Should Avoid Feeding Garlic to Your Pekingese
Garlic belongs to the *Allium* family (which includes onions, leeks, and chives). These plants contain compounds called **thiosulfates**, which are toxic to dogs. 

While humans digest these compounds easily, a Pekingese's system cannot. Instead, thiosulfates cause oxidative damage to the red blood cells, leading them to rupture and destroy themselves. This condition is known as **hemolytic anemia**.

Because Pekingese are small-breed dogs, they are significantly more susceptible to garlic toxicity than larger breeds; even a small amount of garlic can represent a toxic dose relative to their body weight.

---

## The Risks: What Happens if a Pekingese Eats Garlic?

Garlic toxicity is not always immediate; symptoms may take a few days to appear. Be on the lookout for:

*   **Pale Gums:** A sign of anemia (lack of red blood cells).
*   **Lethargy:** Unusual tiredness or weakness.
*   **Digestive Distress:** Vomiting or diarrhea.
*   **Rapid Heart Rate:** The heart works harder to pump the remaining oxygenated blood.
*   **Dark-Colored Urine:** A result of the breakdown of red blood cells.

**Are there any benefits?** 
Contrary to some myths about garlic being a "natural parasite cleanse," the risks of toxicity far outweigh any perceived benefits. Always use vet-approved supplements for parasite control.

---

## What to Do If Your Pekingese Ate Garlic

If your dog accidentally ingested garlic powder, fresh garlic, or a food containing garlic (like pizza or pasta sauce), take the following steps:

1.  **Remove the Source:** Ensure they cannot eat any more of the food.
2.  **Gather Information:** Note how much they ate and when it happened. Garlic powder is more concentrated and therefore more dangerous than fresh garlic.
3.  **Call Your Veterinarian:** Contact your vet or a Pet Poison Helpline immediately. 
4.  **Monitor Symptoms:** Watch for the signs of anemia mentioned above.
5.  **Do Not Induce Vomiting** unless specifically instructed to do so by a medical professional.
