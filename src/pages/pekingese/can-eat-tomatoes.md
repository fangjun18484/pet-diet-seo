# Can a Pekingese Eat Tomatoes?

**Verdict: YES (But only ripe, red tomatoes)**

While ripe tomatoes are generally safe for Pekingese dogs, the green parts of the plant—including stems, leaves, and unripe green tomatoes—are **toxic**.

---

### Why the Distinction Matters
The primary concern with tomatoes is a substance called **solanine**. Solanine is a glycoalkaloid poison found in the Nightshade family. While ripe red tomatoes have negligible levels of solanine, green tomatoes and the tomato vine contain high concentrations that can cause gastrointestinal and neurological distress in small breeds like the Pekingese.

### Potential Benefits & Risks

#### ✅ The Benefits (Ripe Tomatoes)
When fed in moderation, ripe tomatoes provide:
*   **Lycopene:** A powerful antioxidant that supports heart health and cellular repair.
*   **Vitamin C & K:** Essential for immune function and blood clotting.
*   **Potassium:** Supports nerve function and fluid balance.

#### ❌ The Risks
*   **Solanine Toxicity:** If they chew on a garden plant or eat a green tomato, symptoms include lethargy, vomiting, and diarrhea.
*   **Acidity:** Tomatoes are acidic. Some Pekingese may experience an upset stomach or acid reflux if they eat too many.
*   **Added Ingredients:** Many commercial tomato products (ketchup, pasta sauce) contain **onion or garlic powder**, both of which are highly toxic to dogs.

---

### How to Safely Feed Tomatoes to Your Pekingese

Because Pekingese are a small breed prone to weight gain and sensitive stomachs, follow these guidelines:

1.  **Ripe Only:** Ensure the tomato is fully red and soft.
2.  **Remove the Greens:** Strip away all leaves and stems before giving a piece to your dog.
3.  **Small Portions:** Treat tomatoes as a snack, not a meal. A small cube or two is sufficient.
4.  **Fresh is Best:** Never feed your dog canned tomatoes, sauces, or ketchups due to the high sodium and toxic additives (xylitol, onion, garlic).

### What to do if your Pekingese ate a green tomato or plant
If your dog has ingested a tomato plant or an unripe green tomato, monitor them closely for:
*   Excessive drooling
*   Vomiting or diarrhea
*   Muscle weakness or lack of coordination

**If you notice these symptoms, contact your veterinarian or a pet poison control center immediately.**
