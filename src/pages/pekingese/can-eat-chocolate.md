# Can a Pekingese Eat Chocolate?

**Verdict: ❌ NO**

## Why Chocolate is Dangerous for Pekingese
Chocolate contains **methylxanthines**, specifically **theobromine** and **caffeine**. While humans can metabolize these compounds easily, dogs—including Pekingese—process them very slowly. 

Because Pekingese are a small breed, their lower body mass means that even a small amount of dark chocolate or cocoa powder can reach toxic levels in their bloodstream much faster than it would in a larger dog.

## The Risks: What Happens if They Eat It?
There are no benefits to feeding chocolate to a dog. The risks are severe and can include:

*   **Gastrointestinal Distress:** Vomiting and diarrhea.
*   **Hyperactivity:** Restlessness, pacing, and an abnormally fast heart rate.
*   **Neurological Issues:** Muscle tremors, agitation, and in severe cases, seizures.
*   **Cardiac Failure:** Theobromine acts as a stimulant that can overwork the heart, leading to potential heart failure.

**Note:** The darker and more bitter the chocolate (e.g., baking chocolate or cocoa powder), the higher the concentration of theobromine and the more dangerous it is.

## What to Do If Your Pekingese Ate Chocolate
If you suspect your Pekingese has ingested chocolate, **act quickly**. Follow these steps:

1.  **Call Your Vet Immediately:** Contact your veterinarian or an emergency animal clinic right away.
2.  **Identify the Type and Amount:** Determine exactly what kind of chocolate was eaten (White, Milk, Dark, or Baker's) and approximately how much was consumed.
3.  **Save the Packaging:** Bring the wrapper to the vet so they can calculate the exact toxicity levels based on the ingredients.
4.  **Monitor Symptoms:** Watch for excessive panting, shaking, or vomiting while you are en route to the clinic.
5.  **Do Not Induce Vomiting** unless specifically instructed to do so by a veterinary professional.

### Safe Alternatives for Your Pekingese
If you want to give your Pekingese a special treat, stick to dog-safe options such as:
*   **Fresh Blueberries**
*   **Slices of Apple** (no seeds)
*   **Plain Cooked Pumpkin**
*   **Dog-specific "Pupcakes"** (made without cocoa)
