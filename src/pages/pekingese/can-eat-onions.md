# Can a Pekingese Eat Onions?

**Verdict: ❌ NO**

## Why Onions are Dangerous for Pekingese
Onions (along with garlic, chives, and leeks) contain compounds called **N-propyl disulfide**. While these are harmless to humans, they are toxic to dogs. 

When a Pekingese ingests onions, these compounds cause oxidative damage to their red blood cells, leading to a condition called **hemolytic anemia**. This essentially means the red blood cells burst, reducing the blood's ability to carry oxygen throughout the body.

Because Pekingese are a small breed, they are particularly susceptible to toxicity; even a small amount of onion can have a significant impact on their smaller body mass.

## Potential Risks & Symptoms
There are **zero nutritional benefits** to feeding onions to your dog. The risks are severe and can include:

*   **Hemolytic Anemia:** The destruction of red blood cells.
*   **Gastrointestinal Distress:** Vomiting, diarrhea, and abdominal pain.
*   **Lethargy:** Extreme tiredness due to lack of oxygen in the blood.
*   **Pale Gums:** A sign of anemia and reduced circulation.
*   **Dark-Colored Urine:** Often reddish or brown, indicating the presence of hemoglobin.

*Note: Symptoms may not appear immediately and can take several days to manifest after ingestion.*

## What to Do If Your Pekingese Ate Onions
If your Pekingese has consumed onions—whether raw, cooked, or in powder form (like onion powder in seasonings)—**treat this as a medical emergency.**

1.  **Call Your Vet Immediately:** Contact your veterinarian or an emergency animal clinic right away.
2.  **Identify the Amount:** Try to determine how much onion was consumed and when.
3.  **Do Not Induce Vomiting Unless Instructed:** Only induce vomiting if your vet specifically tells you to do so over the phone.
4.  **Monitor for Symptoms:** Keep a close eye on their gum color and energy levels until you reach the clinic.

### Safe Alternatives for Your Pekingese
If you want to give your Pekingese a crunchy or flavorful treat, opt for these dog-safe vegetables instead:
*   **Carrots** (Great for dental health)
*   **Green Beans** (Low calorie)
*   **Cucumber** (Hydrating and refreshing)
*   **Sweet Potato** (Cooked and plain)
