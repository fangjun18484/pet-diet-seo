# Can a Cane Corso Eat Garlic?

**Verdict: ❌ NO**

While your Cane Corso may be a powerful guardian, their digestive system cannot handle garlic. **You should not feed garlic to your Cane Corso.**

---

## Why is Garlic Dangerous for Cane Corsos?
Garlic belongs to the *Allium* family (along with onions, shallots, and chives). These plants contain compounds called **thiosulfates**. 

Unlike humans, dogs lack the enzyme necessary to process these compounds. When a dog ingests garlic, the thiosulfates cause oxidative damage to the red blood cells, leading to a condition called **hemolytic anemia**, where the body destroys its own red blood cells faster than it can produce them.

---

## Potential Risks & Symptoms
Because Cane Corsos are large-breed dogs, they may tolerate a tiny amount of garlic powder in a treat better than a small toy breed would. However, toxicity is cumulative and unpredictable. 

**Watch for these signs of garlic poisoning:**
*   **Pale Gums:** A sign of anemia (lack of oxygen in the blood).
*   **Lethargy:** Unusual tiredness or weakness.
*   **Rapid Heart Rate:** The heart works harder to pump the remaining red blood cells.
*   **Dark-Colored Urine:** A result of the breakdown of red blood cells.
*   **Loss of Appetite:** General nausea or digestive upset.

---

## What to Do If Your Cane Corso Ate Garlic
If your dog accidentally swallowed a piece of garlic or a garlic-heavy food (like a garlic knot or seasoned meat), do not panic, but act quickly.

### 1. Assess the Amount
A small amount of garlic powder in a commercial dog treat is usually processed to be safe. However, raw garlic, garlic powder, or concentrated garlic oil is highly dangerous.

### 2. Contact Your Vet
Call your veterinarian or an animal poison control center immediately. Provide them with:
*   The approximate amount of garlic consumed.
*   When it was eaten.
*   Your dog's current weight.

### 3. Monitor for Symptoms
Symptoms of anemia may not appear immediately; they can take **several days** to manifest. Keep a close eye on your dog's energy levels and gum color for the next 48–72 hours.

### 4. Avoid "Home Remedies"
Do not attempt to induce vomiting unless specifically instructed to do so by a licensed veterinary professional.
