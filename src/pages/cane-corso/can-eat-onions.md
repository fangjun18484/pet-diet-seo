# Can a Cane Corso Eat Onions?

**Verdict: ❌ NO**

Cane Corsos, like all dog breeds, **should never eat onions**. Whether raw, cooked, powdered, or dried, onions are toxic to dogs and can lead to serious health complications.

---

### Why Are Onions Dangerous for Cane Corsos?
Onions belong to the *Allium* family. They contain compounds called **N-propyl disulfides**, which cause oxidative damage to a dog's red blood cells. 

This process leads to **hemolytic anemia**, a condition where the body destroys its own red blood cells faster than it can produce them. While a large breed like the Cane Corso may require a larger quantity of onions to show immediate symptoms compared to a small dog, the toxicity is cumulative and dangerous regardless of the dog's size.

---

### Potential Risks & Symptoms
There are no nutritional benefits to feeding onions to your dog; there are only severe risks. If your Cane Corso consumes onions, keep a close eye out for these signs of toxicity:

*   **Pale Gums:** A sign of anemia (lack of oxygen in the blood).
*   **Lethargy:** Unusual tiredness or weakness.
*   **Dark-Colored Urine:** Often reddish or brown due to the breakdown of red blood cells.
*   **Loss of Appetite:** Refusing food or treats.
*   **Rapid Heart Rate:** The heart works harder to compensate for the loss of red blood cells.

---

### What to Do If Your Cane Corso Ate Onions
If you suspect your dog has ingested onions (including onion powder in human foods), **act quickly**:

1.  **Contact Your Vet Immediately:** Call your veterinarian or a Pet Poison Helpline. Provide them with the estimated amount and the form (raw vs. powdered) of the onion consumed.
2.  **Do Not Induce Vomiting Unless Instructed:** Only induce vomiting if your vet specifically tells you to do so.
3.  **Monitor Behavior:** Note any changes in energy levels or gum color to help your vet make a diagnosis.
4.  **Blood Tests:** Your vet may perform a CBC (Complete Blood Count) to check for anemia and determine if a blood transfusion or supportive care is necessary.

**Pro Tip:** Be careful with "hidden" onions in foods like beef stew, rotisserie chicken seasoning, and certain types of bread or crackers!
