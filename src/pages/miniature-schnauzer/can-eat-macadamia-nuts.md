# Can Miniature Schnauzers Eat Macadamia Nuts?

**Verdict: ❌ NO**

## Why You Should Never Feed Macadamia Nuts to Your Dog
Macadamia nuts are **toxic to dogs**, including Miniature Schnauzers. Unlike some foods that are only dangerous in large quantities, macadamia nuts can cause a severe reaction even in small amounts. 

While the exact toxin has not been fully identified, these nuts trigger a metabolic reaction that affects the dog's nervous system and overall health.

---

## The Risks: What Happens if They Eat One?

For a Miniature Schnauzer, the risks are twofold: **toxicity** and **pancreatitis**.

### 1. Macadamia Toxicity Symptoms
If your dog ingests macadamia nuts, you may notice these signs within 12 hours:
*   **Lethargy:** Sudden extreme tiredness or weakness.
*   **Hind-leg Weakness:** Difficulty walking or "wobbly" back legs.
*   **Vomiting & Diarrhea:** Gastrointestinal distress.
*   **Hyperthermia:** An increase in body temperature.
*   **Tremors:** In severe cases, muscle tremors or seizures.

### 2. The "Schnauzer Factor": Pancreatitis
Miniature Schnauzers are genetically predisposed to **hyperlipidemia** (high blood fats) and **pancreatitis**. Because macadamia nuts are incredibly high in fat, they can trigger a painful inflammation of the pancreas, even if the toxic effects are mild.

---

## What to Do If Your Miniature Schnauzer Ate Macadamia Nuts

If you suspect your dog has consumed macadamia nuts, **do not wait for symptoms to appear.** Take the following steps immediately:

1.  **Remove Access:** Ensure no more nuts are available.
2.  **Call Your Vet:** Contact your veterinarian or an emergency pet clinic immediately.
3.  **Animal Poison Control:** If your vet is unavailable, call the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at **(888) 426-4435**.
4.  **Gather Information:** Try to determine how many nuts were eaten and when it happened to help the vet provide the correct treatment.

### Safe Alternatives for Your Schnauzer
If you want to give your Miniature Schnauzer a crunchy, healthy treat, try these dog-safe options instead:
*   **Carrot sticks** (Low calorie, great for teeth)
*   **Apple slices** (No seeds or core)
*   **Blueberries** (Antioxidant-rich)
*   **Plain cucumber** (Hydrating and safe)
