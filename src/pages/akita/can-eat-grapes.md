# Can Akitas Eat Grapes? 🚫

**Verdict: NO.**

**Akitas, like all dog breeds, must never eat grapes or raisins.**

---

### Why Grapes are Dangerous for Akitas
While many fruits are healthy treats, grapes and raisins are highly toxic to dogs. Even a small amount can trigger **acute kidney failure**. 

Although the exact toxin in grapes was long debated, recent research suggests that **tartaric acid** is the primary culprit. Because every dog reacts differently, there is no "safe" dose; some dogs may be fine with one grape, while others may suffer severe organ failure from a single piece.

### The Risks: What Happens if an Akita Eats Grapes?
Because Akitas are large, powerful dogs, owners sometimes mistakenly believe their size protects them from toxicity. This is false. The risks include:

*   **Acute Renal Failure:** The most severe risk, where the kidneys stop filtering waste from the blood.
*   **Gastrointestinal Distress:** Sudden vomiting and diarrhea.
*   **Lethargy:** Extreme tiredness or weakness.
*   **Loss of Appetite:** A sudden refusal to eat.
*   **Dehydration:** Increased thirst or, conversely, a complete stop in urination (a sign of kidney failure).

### What to Do If Your Akita Ate Grapes
If your Akita has ingested grapes or raisins—whether fresh, dried, or in a food product (like raisin bread)—**treat this as a medical emergency.**

1.  **Call Your Vet Immediately:** Contact your primary veterinarian or the nearest emergency animal clinic.
2.  **Contact Poison Control:** You can also call the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center.
3.  **Do Not Induce Vomiting Unless Instructed:** Only induce vomiting if your vet specifically tells you to do so over the phone.
4.  **Monitor Symptoms:** Note exactly how many grapes were eaten and how long ago it happened to help your vet determine the treatment plan.

### Safe Alternatives for Your Akita
Want to give your Akita a fruity treat instead? Try these dog-safe options:
*   **Blueberries** (Rich in antioxidants)
*   **Apple Slices** (Seedless and core-free)
*   **Watermelon** (Seedless and rind-free)
*   **Banana** (In moderation)
