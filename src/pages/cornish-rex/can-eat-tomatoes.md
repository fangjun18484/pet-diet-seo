# Can a Cornish Rex Eat Tomatoes? 🍅

**Verdict: YES (But only fully ripe ones!)**

While a small piece of a **ripe** tomato is generally safe for a Cornish Rex, you must be extremely cautious. The danger lies in the parts of the plant that are not yet ripe.

---

## The Quick Explanation
Ripe red tomatoes are non-toxic to cats. However, the tomato plant—including the **stems, leaves, and green (unripe) tomatoes**—contains a compound called **solanine**. Solanine is toxic to cats and can lead to serious health issues if ingested.

Because Cornish Rex cats are known for their high energy and curious nature, it is vital to ensure they cannot chew on home-grown tomato plants.

---

## Potential Benefits & Risks

### ✅ The Benefits (In Moderation)
If your cat enjoys a tiny piece of ripe tomato, they may benefit from:
*   **Lycopene:** A powerful antioxidant.
*   **Hydration:** Tomatoes have high water content.
*   **Vitamins:** Small amounts of Vitamin C and Potassium.

### ⚠️ The Risks
*   **Solanine Poisoning:** Ingesting green parts can cause gastrointestinal distress, lethargy, and heart arrhythmia.
*   **Digestive Upset:** Some cats have sensitive stomachs; tomatoes can cause diarrhea or vomiting.
*   **Acidity:** The natural acidity in tomatoes may be too harsh for some cats' digestive tracts.

---

## How to Safely Feed Tomatoes to Your Cornish Rex

If you want to give your Cornish Rex a taste of tomato, follow these strict safety guidelines:

1.  **Ripe Only:** Only feed the fleshy, red part of the tomato. **Never** feed green tomatoes.
2.  **Remove the "Danger Zones":** Completely remove all stems, leaves, and seeds.
3.  **Keep it Tiny:** Treat it as a rare snack, not a meal. A piece the size of a blueberry is plenty.
4.  **Plain is Best:** Never feed your cat "seasoned" tomatoes. Avoid anything containing salt, garlic, onions, or sugar (like ketchup or pasta sauce), as these are toxic to cats.

### 🚨 What to do if your cat ate a green tomato or plant:
If you suspect your Cornish Rex has eaten a tomato leaf, stem, or an unripe green tomato, **contact your veterinarian immediately**. Watch for these signs of solanine poisoning:
*   Excessive drooling
*   Vomiting or diarrhea
*   Lack of coordination (ataxia)
*   Extreme lethargy
