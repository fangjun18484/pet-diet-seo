# Can a Cornish Rex Eat Onions?

**Verdict: ❌ NO**

## Why Onions Are Dangerous for Cornish Rex Cats
Regardless of their unique curly coat and breed characteristics, a Cornish Rex is a cat, and onions are **toxic to all felines**. 

Onions (along with garlic, leeks, and chives) contain compounds called **N-propyl disulfides**. Cats lack the specific enzyme needed to break down these compounds. When ingested, these toxins cause oxidative damage to the red blood cells, leading to a serious condition called **hemolytic anemia**, where the body destroys its own red blood cells faster than it can produce them.

## Potential Risks & Symptoms
There are **zero nutritional benefits** to feeding onions to a cat. The risks are severe and can be fatal if left untreated.

### Immediate to Short-Term Risks:
*   **Gastrointestinal Distress:** Vomiting and diarrhea.
*   **Lethargy:** Unusual tiredness or weakness.
*   **Loss of Appetite:** Refusing food or water.

### Severe Clinical Signs (Hemolytic Anemia):
*   **Pale Gums:** A sign of decreased oxygen in the blood.
*   **Dark-Colored Urine:** Reddish or brown urine (hemoglobinuria).
*   **Rapid Heart Rate:** The heart works harder to compensate for the lack of red blood cells.
*   **Difficulty Breathing:** Shortness of breath due to anemia.

## What to Do If Your Cornish Rex Ate Onions
If your cat has ingested raw, cooked, or powdered onion (including onion powder in human snacks), **treat this as a medical emergency.**

1.  **Contact Your Veterinarian Immediately:** Call your vet or an emergency animal clinic right away.
2.  **Identify the Amount:** Try to determine how much was eaten and whether it was raw or concentrated (powdered forms are often more toxic).
3.  **Do Not Induce Vomiting:** Never attempt to make your cat vomit unless specifically instructed to do so by a licensed veterinary professional.
4.  **Monitor Closely:** Be alert for the symptoms mentioned above, as some signs of anemia may not appear for several days after ingestion.

**Pro Tip:** Always check the labels of "cat-friendly" human treats or homemade toppers. Many broths and seasonings contain onion powder, which is highly concentrated and dangerous for your pet.
