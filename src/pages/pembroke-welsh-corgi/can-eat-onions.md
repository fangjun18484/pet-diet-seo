# Can a Pembroke Welsh Corgi Eat Onions?

**Verdict: ❌ NO**

## Why Onions are Dangerous for Corgis
Regardless of the breed, onions are **toxic to dogs**, including Pembroke Welsh Corgis. Onions contain compounds called **N-propyl disulfides**, which cause oxidative damage to a dog's red blood cells. 

This process leads to a condition called **hemolytic anemia**, where the body destroys its own red blood cells faster than it can produce them, severely limiting the blood's ability to carry oxygen to the organs.

---

## The Risks: What Happens if Your Corgi Eats Onions?
There are no nutritional benefits to feeding onions to a dog; there are only significant health risks. The danger applies to **raw, cooked, powdered, or dehydrated onions**.

### Potential Symptoms of Onion Poisoning:
*   **Pale Gums:** A sign of anemia due to lack of red blood cells.
*   **Lethargy:** Extreme tiredness or weakness.
*   **Loss of Appetite:** Refusing food or treats.
*   **Dark-Colored Urine:** Often appearing reddish-brown due to hemoglobinuria.
*   **Rapid Heart Rate:** The heart works harder to compensate for low oxygen levels.

*Note: Symptoms may not appear immediately and can take several days to manifest.*

---

## What to Do if Your Corgi Ate Onions
If your Pembroke Welsh Corgi has ingested onions (or a food containing onion powder, like certain broths or seasonings), **act quickly**.

### 1. Contact Your Veterinarian Immediately
Call your vet or a local emergency animal clinic. Be prepared to tell them:
*   How much was eaten.
*   What form the onion was in (raw vs. powdered).
*   When the ingestion occurred.

### 2. Do Not Induce Vomiting Unless Instructed
Never try to make your dog vomit without professional guidance, as this can sometimes cause further complications.

### 3. Monitor for Warning Signs
Keep a close eye on your Corgi’s energy levels and gum color over the next 48–72 hours, even if they seem fine initially.

### Safe Alternatives for Your Corgi
If you want to give your Corgi a crunchy, healthy treat, swap onions for:
*   **Carrots** (Great for teething and low calorie)
*   **Cucumber slices** (Hydrating and refreshing)
*   **Green beans** (A healthy, low-calorie snack)
