# Can a Shiba Inu Eat Macadamia Nuts?

**Verdict: ❌ NO**

## Why Macadamia Nuts Are Dangerous for Shiba Inus
Regardless of the breed, **macadamia nuts are toxic to dogs**, including Shiba Inus. These nuts contain an unidentified compound that triggers a reaction in a dog's nervous system, leading to a condition known as **macadamia nut toxicosis**. 

Because Shiba Inus are typically small to medium-sized dogs, even a small amount of these nuts can lead to a significant toxic reaction.

---

## Potential Risks & Symptoms
There are **zero nutritional benefits** for a Shiba Inu to eat macadamia nuts; the risks far outweigh any perceived gain. If your dog consumes macadamia nuts, you may notice the following symptoms within 12 hours:

*   **Rear Leg Weakness:** Difficulty walking or "wobbly" hind legs.
*   **Lethargy:** Extreme tiredness or sudden lack of energy.
*   **Vomiting:** Nausea and digestive upset.
*   **Hyperthermia:** An increase in body temperature.
*   **Tremors:** In severe cases, muscle tremors or disorientation.

---

## What to Do If Your Shiba Inu Ate Macadamia Nuts
If your Shiba Inu has accidentally ingested macadamia nuts, **treat this as a medical emergency.** Follow these steps immediately:

1.  **Remove the Source:** Ensure your dog cannot eat any more of the nuts.
2.  **Call Your Vet:** Contact your veterinarian or a local emergency pet clinic immediately.
3.  **Provide Details:** Be ready to tell the vet **how many** nuts were eaten and **how long ago** the ingestion occurred.
4.  **Monitor Closely:** Keep a close eye on their mobility and energy levels while transporting them to the clinic.
5.  **Do Not Induce Vomiting:** Never induce vomiting unless specifically instructed to do so by a licensed veterinary professional.

### Safe Alternatives for Your Shiba
If you want to give your Shiba Inu a crunchy, healthy treat, try these dog-safe options instead:
*   **Carrot sticks** (Great for chewing)
*   **Apple slices** (Seedless and skinless)
*   **Plain blueberries** (Antioxidant-rich)
*   **Cucumber slices** (Low calorie and hydrating)
