# Can a Soft Coated Wheaten Terrier Eat Macadamia Nuts?

**Verdict: ❌ NO**

## Why Macadamia Nuts Are Dangerous for Dogs
Regardless of the breed, **macadamia nuts are toxic to all dogs**, including the Soft Coated Wheaten Terrier. While many nuts are harmless or mildly risky, macadamia nuts contain a specific toxin (though the exact chemical compound is still being studied) that affects a dog's nervous system and metabolism.

Because Wheaten Terriers are active and curious, they may be tempted to sneak a snack from the counter, but even a small amount can lead to significant health issues.

## Potential Risks & Symptoms
Eating macadamia nuts can lead to **macadamia nut toxicosis**. The onset of symptoms usually occurs within 12 hours of ingestion. Keep a close eye on your dog for the following:

*   **Hind Limb Weakness:** Difficulty walking or "stumbling."
*   **Lethargy:** Unusual tiredness or lack of energy.
*   **Vomiting:** Gastrointestinal distress.
*   **Hyperthermia:** An increase in body temperature.
*   **Tremors:** In severe cases, muscle shaking or seizures.

**Are there any benefits?**
No. There are zero nutritional benefits for a dog to eat macadamia nuts; the risks far outweigh any perceived protein or fat gains.

## What to Do If Your Wheaten Terrier Ate Macadamia Nuts
If you suspect your dog has ingested macadamia nuts, **act quickly**. This is a veterinary emergency.

1.  **Remove Access:** Immediately take away any remaining nuts to prevent further ingestion.
2.  **Call Your Vet:** Contact your veterinarian or an emergency animal clinic immediately.
3.  **Contact Poison Control:** You can also call the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at **(888) 426-4435**.
4.  **Provide Details:** Be ready to tell the vet how many nuts were eaten, the approximate time of ingestion, and your dog's current weight.
5.  **Monitor:** Watch for the symptoms listed above while you are en route to the clinic.

### Safe Alternatives for Your Wheaten Terrier
If you want to give your pup a crunchy, healthy treat, try these dog-safe options instead:
*   **Plain Carrot Sticks** (Great for chewing)
*   **Apple Slices** (Without seeds or core)
*   **Blueberries** (Antioxidant-rich)
*   **Plain Cucumber** (Low calorie and hydrating)
