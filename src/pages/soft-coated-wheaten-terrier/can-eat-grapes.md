# Can a Soft Coated Wheaten Terrier Eat Grapes?

## The Verdict: ❌ NO

**No, a Soft Coated Wheaten Terrier cannot eat grapes.** Grapes and raisins are highly toxic to all dog breeds, regardless of their size or health status.

---

## Why Grapes are Dangerous for Your Wheaten
While some dogs can tolerate grapes without immediate symptoms, many others experience a severe reaction. Recent research suggests that **tartaric acid** found in grapes is the primary cause of toxicity. 

For a Soft Coated Wheaten Terrier, consuming even a small amount of grapes or raisins can lead to **acute kidney failure**, a life-threatening condition where the kidneys suddenly stop functioning.

---

## Potential Risks & Symptoms
There are **no nutritional benefits** to feeding grapes to your dog; the risks far outweigh any perceived advantage. 

### Warning Signs of Grape Toxicity:
If your Wheaten has ingested grapes, watch for these critical symptoms:
*   **Vomiting and Diarrhea:** Usually occurs within a few hours.
*   **Lethargy:** Unusual tiredness or weakness.
*   **Loss of Appetite:** Refusing their favorite treats or meals.
*   **Dehydration:** Increased thirst or, conversely, a decrease in urine output (a sign of kidney failure).
*   **Disorientation:** Appearing confused or unstable on their feet.

---

## What to Do If Your Dog Ate Grapes
Because grape toxicity can strike quickly and unpredictably, **this is a medical emergency.**

1.  **Call Your Veterinarian Immediately:** Do not wait for symptoms to appear. Early intervention is key to preventing permanent kidney damage.
2.  **Contact Pet Poison Control:** If your vet is unavailable, call a dedicated pet poison hotline.
3.  **Do Not Induce Vomiting Unless Advised:** Only induce vomiting if specifically instructed to do so by a veterinary professional.
4.  **Provide Information:** Be prepared to tell your vet how many grapes were eaten, the variety of grape, and approximately how long ago the ingestion occurred.

### Safe Alternatives for Your Wheaten
Want to give your Soft Coated Wheaten Terrier a healthy, fruity snack? Try these dog-safe options instead:
*   **Blueberries** (Rich in antioxidants)
*   **Apple Slices** (Seedless and core-free)
*   **Watermelon** (Seedless and rind-free)
*   **Banana** (In moderation)
