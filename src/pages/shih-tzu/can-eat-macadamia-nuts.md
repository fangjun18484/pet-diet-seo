# Can a Shih Tzu Eat Macadamia Nuts?

**Verdict: ❌ NO**

## Why Macadamia Nuts Are Dangerous for Shih Tzus
You should **never** feed macadamia nuts to a Shih Tzu. Unlike some nuts that are simply hard to digest, macadamia nuts contain a specific toxin that is poisonous to dogs. 

Because Shih Tzus are small breed dogs, even a small amount of these nuts can lead to a significant toxic reaction due to their lower body mass.

---

## The Risks: What Happens if They Eat One?
Macadamia nuts target a dog's nervous system. While the exact toxin is not fully understood, the clinical effects are well-documented. 

**Potential symptoms of macadamia nut poisoning include:**
*   **Backend Weakness:** Difficulty walking or "wobbly" hind legs.
*   **Vomiting & Diarrhea:** Gastrointestinal distress shortly after ingestion.
*   **Lethargy:** Extreme tiredness or depression.
*   **Hyperthermia:** An increase in body temperature.
*   **Tremors:** In severe cases, muscle tremors or seizures may occur.

**Are there any benefits?** 
No. There are zero nutritional benefits for a Shih Tzu that outweigh the severe toxicity risks.

---

## What to Do if Your Shih Tzu Ate Macadamia Nuts
If your dog has accidentally ingested macadamia nuts (or a product containing them, like certain cookies or granola bars), **act quickly.**

1.  **Call Your Vet Immediately:** Contact your veterinarian or a pet poison control center right away.
2.  **Identify the Amount:** Try to determine exactly how many nuts were eaten and when.
3.  **Monitor Symptoms:** Watch for the signs of toxicity listed above.
4.  **Do NOT Induce Vomiting:** Unless specifically instructed to do so by a licensed veterinarian, do not try to make your dog vomit, as this can sometimes cause further complications.

### Safe Alternatives for Your Shih Tzu
If you want to give your Shih Tzu a crunchy, healthy treat, try these dog-safe options instead:
*   **Blueberries** (Antioxidant-rich)
*   **Carrot slices** (Great for dental health)
*   **Apple slices** (Seedless and skinless)
*   **Plain cooked pumpkin** (Great for digestion)
