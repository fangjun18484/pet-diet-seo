# Can a Shih Tzu Eat Tomatoes?

**Verdict: YES (But only if they are fully ripe)**

While a piece of a ripe red tomato is generally safe for Shih Tzus, you must be extremely cautious about the part of the plant your dog is consuming.

---

### Why the Caution?
The primary concern is a compound called **solanine**. Solanine is a natural toxin found in the green parts of the tomato plant (stems, leaves, and unripened green tomatoes). While the red, ripe flesh of the fruit is safe, ingesting solanine can lead to toxicity in small breeds like Shih Tzus.

---

### Potential Benefits & Risks

#### ✅ The Benefits (Ripe Tomatoes Only)
In moderation, ripe tomatoes provide several nutrients that can support your Shih Tzu's health:
*   **Vitamin C & K:** Supports the immune system and bone health.
*   **Potassium:** Helps maintain healthy nerve and muscle function.
*   **Lycopene:** A powerful antioxidant that helps fight free radicals.

#### ⚠️ The Risks
*   **Solanine Poisoning:** Consuming green tomatoes or plant stems can cause lethargy, vomiting, diarrhea, and heart rate irregularities.
*   **Acidity:** Tomatoes are acidic. Some Shih Tzus have sensitive stomachs and may experience gastrointestinal upset if they eat too many.
*   **Added Ingredients:** Never feed your dog "tomato products" (ketchup, pasta sauce, or salsa), as these often contain garlic or onion powder, which are toxic to dogs.

---

### How to Safely Feed Tomatoes to Your Shih Tzu

If you want to give your Shih Tzu a taste of tomato, follow these safety guidelines:

1.  **Check the Color:** Only feed tomatoes that are **deep red and fully ripe**.
2.  **Remove the Greens:** Strictly remove all stems, leaves, and any green parts of the fruit.
3.  **Portion Control:** Since Shih Tzus are small, treat tomatoes as a rare snack, not a meal. A small cube (roughly 1/2 inch) is plenty.
4.  **Introduce Slowly:** Give a tiny piece first to ensure your dog doesn't have an adverse allergic reaction or stomach upset.

### What to do if your Shih Tzu ate a green tomato?
If your dog has chewed on a tomato plant or eaten a green tomato, watch for signs of **solanine poisoning**:
*   Extreme lethargy
*   Vomiting or diarrhea
*   Muscle weakness or tremors

**If you notice these symptoms, contact your veterinarian or an emergency pet clinic immediately.**
