# Can a Shih Tzu Eat Cheese? The Quick Guide for Pet Parents

**Verdict: YES (In Moderation)**

While most Shih Tzus can safely enjoy a small piece of cheese, it should be treated as an **occasional treat**, not a staple of their diet.

---

### Why the Caution?
Most dogs are lactose intolerant to some degree. While cheese has less lactose than milk, it is still rich in fats and sodium. Because Shih Tzus are a small breed, they are prone to rapid weight gain and digestive upsets if given too many rich treats.

### Potential Benefits vs. Risks

| **Potential Benefits** | **Potential Risks** |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Protein & Calcium:** Provides essential nutrients for bone health. | **Lactose Intolerance:** Can lead to gas, bloating, or diarrhea. |
| **High Palatability:** Great for hiding pills or rewarding training. | **Weight Gain:** High calorie density can lead to obesity. |
| **Quick Energy:** Provides a fast source of energy in small amounts. | **Sodium Overload:** Too much salt can strain a small dog's kidneys. |

---

### How to Safely Feed Cheese to Your Shih Tzu

If you want to share a snack with your pup, follow these professional guidelines to keep them healthy:

#### 1. Choose the Right Type
*   **Best Choices:** Low-sodium cottage cheese, mozzarella, or mild cheddar.
*   **Avoid Completely:** 
    *   **Blue Cheese/Brie:** May contain molds toxic to dogs.
    *   **Seasoned Cheeses:** Avoid any cheese containing garlic, onion, or chives.
    *   **High-Salt Cheeses:** Avoid heavily processed or aged salty cheeses.

#### 2. Watch the Portion Size
A Shih Tzu is small! Limit cheese to a **tiny cube (about the size of a fingernail)** once or twice a week. Cheese should never exceed 10% of their daily caloric intake.

#### 3. The "Test Drive"
The first time you give your Shih Tzu cheese, offer a tiny crumb and wait 24 hours. Watch for signs of digestive distress, such as:
*   Excessive gas
*   Loose stools
*   Vomiting

**Pro Tip:** If your Shih Tzu has a history of pancreatitis or is overweight, consult your vet before introducing cheese into their diet.
