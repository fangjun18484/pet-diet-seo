# Can Cavalier King Charles Spaniels Eat Onions?

**Verdict: ❌ NO**

Cavalier King Charles Spaniels, like all dog breeds, **cannot** eat onions. Onions are toxic to dogs and can lead to serious health complications.

---

### Why are Onions Dangerous for Dogs?
Onions belong to the *Allium* family (which also includes garlic, chives, and leeks). They contain compounds called **N-propyl disulfide**, which are toxic to canine red blood cells. 

When a dog ingests these compounds, it causes oxidative damage to the red blood cells, leading them to rupture. This condition is known as **hemolytic anemia**. Because Cavalier King Charles Spaniels are small dogs, even a relatively small amount of onion can have a significant impact on their system.

---

### Potential Risks & Symptoms
There are no nutritional benefits to feeding onions to your dog; there are only severe risks. Whether the onion is **raw, cooked, powdered, or dehydrated**, the toxicity remains.

**Watch for these signs of onion poisoning:**
*   **Pale Gums:** A sign of anemia (lack of red blood cells).
*   **Lethargy:** Unusual tiredness or weakness.
*   **Dark-Colored Urine:** Often reddish or brown due to hemoglobinuria.
*   **Loss of Appetite:** Refusing food or treats.
*   **Rapid Heart Rate:** The body attempting to compensate for low oxygen levels in the blood.

---

### What to Do If Your Cavalier Ate Onions
If your dog accidentally ingested onions or a food product containing onion powder (like certain broths or seasonings), **act quickly.**

1.  **Contact Your Vet Immediately:** Call your veterinarian or a pet poison control center right away.
2.  **Identify the Amount:** Try to determine how much was eaten and whether it was raw or concentrated (powder).
3.  **Do Not Induce Vomiting Unless Instructed:** Only induce vomiting if your vet specifically tells you to do so.
4.  **Monitor Symptoms:** Keep a close eye on your dog's gum color and energy levels while waiting for professional help.

**Safe Alternatives:** If you want to give your Cavalier a crunchy, savory treat, try **sliced carrots, cucumber, or a small piece of plain apple (without seeds).**
