# Can a St. Bernard Eat Grapes?

**Verdict: ❌ NO**

## Why You Should Never Feed Grapes to Your St. Bernard
Despite their massive size, St. Bernards are just as susceptible to grape toxicity as any other dog breed. Grapes and raisins contain substances (specifically tartaric acid) that can cause **acute kidney failure** in dogs. 

Because toxicity levels vary by individual dog, there is no "safe amount." Even a single grape can be fatal for some dogs, regardless of their weight.

---

## The Risks: What Happens if a St. Bernard Eats Grapes?
Feeding grapes to your dog isn't just a dietary mistake; it is a medical emergency. The primary risks include:

*   **Acute Renal Failure:** The most severe risk, where the kidneys stop functioning.
*   **Gastrointestinal Distress:** Severe vomiting and diarrhea.
*   **Lethargy:** Sudden loss of energy and weakness.
*   **Dehydration:** Due to the inability of the kidneys to process fluids.
*   **Loss of Appetite:** Refusing food or water.

---

## What to Do if Your St. Bernard Ate Grapes
If your dog has ingested grapes or raisins—whether it was one or a handful—**act immediately.**

1.  **Call Your Vet Immediately:** Contact your veterinarian or the nearest emergency animal clinic.
2.  **Contact Pet Poison Control:** If your vet is unavailable, call the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center.
3.  **Do Not Induce Vomiting Unless Instructed:** Only induce vomiting if your vet tells you to do so over the phone.
4.  **Monitor Symptoms:** Be prepared to tell the vet exactly how many grapes were eaten and how long ago it happened.

---

## Safe, Dog-Friendly Alternatives
If you want to give your St. Bernard a healthy, juicy treat, swap grapes for these vet-approved fruits:

*   **Blueberries:** Packed with antioxidants and safe in moderation.
*   **Apple Slices:** (Remove the seeds and core first).
*   **Watermelon:** (Remove the seeds and rind).
*   **Banana:** A great source of potassium and energy.
