# Can Norwegian Forest Cats Eat Cheese?

**Verdict: YES (But only in moderation)**

While Norwegian Forest Cats can technically eat cheese, it should be treated as an occasional treat rather than a dietary staple.

---

## Why the Caution?
Most adult cats, including the sturdy Norwegian Forest Cat, are **lactose intolerant**. They lack the enzyme *lactase* needed to break down the sugars found in dairy. While cheese has lower lactose levels than a bowl of milk, it can still cause digestive distress for many cats.

## Potential Benefits & Risks

### The Risks ⚠️
*   **Digestive Upset:** The most common reaction is gastrointestinal distress, including diarrhea, gas, and vomiting.
*   **Weight Gain:** Cheese is calorie-dense and high in fat, which can lead to obesity—a concern for large breeds if not monitored.
*   **Sodium Levels:** Many cheeses are high in salt, which can be harmful to a cat's kidneys if consumed in large quantities.
*   **Toxic Additives:** Some gourmet cheeses contain garlic, onion, or certain herbs that are **toxic** to cats.

### The Benefits ✅
*   **Protein & Calcium:** In very small amounts, cheese provides a boost of protein and calcium.
*   **Palatability:** For cats that tolerate dairy, cheese is a high-value treat that can be used for bonding or hiding medication.

---

## How to Safely Feed Cheese to Your Wegie

If you want to give your Norwegian Forest Cat a taste of cheese, follow these safety guidelines:

### 1. Choose the Right Type
Opt for hard, aged cheeses, as they contain significantly less lactose than soft cheeses.
*   **Best Choices:** Cheddar, Parmesan, or Swiss.
*   **Avoid:** Blue cheese, Brie, and processed "cheese spreads" with artificial additives.

### 2. Keep Portions Tiny
A "treat" should be the size of a **small pea**. Your cat does not need the extra calories or fats found in dairy.

### 3. Check the Ingredients
Ensure the cheese is plain. Avoid any varieties containing:
*   Garlic or Onion powder
*   Excessive salt
*   Xylitol (artificial sweetener)

### 4. The "Test Run"
The first time you offer cheese, give a tiny piece and monitor your cat for 24 hours. If you notice any loose stools or lethargy, discontinue cheese immediately.

**Pro Tip:** If your Norwegian Forest Cat loves the taste but has a sensitive stomach, consider **lactose-free** cheese alternatives specifically made for pets.
