# Can a Havanese Eat Macadamia Nuts?

**Verdict: ❌ NO**

**Macadamia nuts are toxic to dogs, including Havanese.** You should never feed them to your pet, and you must ensure they do not have access to them in your home.

---

## Why Macadamia Nuts Are Dangerous for Havanese
While many nuts are harmless or mildly risky, macadamia nuts contain a specific toxin (though the exact compound is not fully understood) that targets a dog's nervous system. 

Because Havanese are small dogs, even a small amount of macadamia nuts can have a significant impact on their body weight compared to a larger breed, making them potentially more susceptible to acute toxicity.

---

## Potential Risks and Symptoms
If your Havanese consumes macadamia nuts, they may experience **Macadamia Nut Toxicosis**. Symptoms typically appear within 12 hours and can include:

*   **Hind Limb Weakness:** Difficulty walking or "wobbly" back legs.
*   **Vomiting:** Frequent nausea and stomach upset.
*   **Lethargy:** Unusual tiredness or depression.
*   **Hyperthermia:** An increase in body temperature (fever).
*   **Tremors:** In severe cases, muscle tremors or disorientation.

**Note:** There are no nutritional benefits to macadamia nuts for dogs; the risks far outweigh any perceived protein or fat gains.

---

## What to Do If Your Havanese Ate Macadamia Nuts
If you suspect your dog has ingested macadamia nuts, **act quickly**. Do not wait for symptoms to appear.

1.  **Call Your Veterinarian Immediately:** Contact your vet or an emergency animal clinic.
2.  **Contact Pet Poison Control:** You can call the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at **(888) 426-4435**.
3.  **Gather Information:** Try to determine how many nuts were eaten and when it happened. Bring the packaging to the vet if possible.
4.  **Do Not Induce Vomiting:** Unless specifically instructed to do so by a veterinary professional, do not try to make your dog vomit at home.

### Safe Alternatives for Your Havanese
If you want to give your Havanese a crunchy, healthy treat, try these dog-safe options instead:
*   **Blueberries** (Antioxidant-rich)
*   **Apple slices** (Seedless and skin-on)
*   **Carrot sticks** (Great for dental health)
*   **Plain cooked pumpkin** (Excellent for digestion)
