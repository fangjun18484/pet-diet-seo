# Can a Somali Cat Eat Garlic?

**Verdict: ❌ NO**

## Why Garlic is Dangerous for Somali Cats
While your Somali cat is curious and adventurous, garlic is **toxic** to all feline breeds. Garlic belongs to the *Allium* family (which includes onions, chives, and leeks). 

Cats lack the specific enzyme needed to break down **thiosulphate**, a compound found in garlic. When ingested, this compound causes oxidative damage to the red blood cells, leading them to rupture and burst. This condition is known as **hemolytic anemia**.

## The Risks: What Happens if They Eat Garlic?
Even small amounts of garlic—whether raw, cooked, or powdered—can be dangerous. Because cats are small, they are more susceptible to toxicity than larger animals.

**Potential symptoms of garlic poisoning include:**
*   **Lethargy:** Unusual tiredness or weakness.
*   **Pale Gums:** A sign of anemia (lack of healthy red blood cells).
*   **Rapid Breathing:** Difficulty getting enough oxygen into the bloodstream.
*   **Loss of Appetite:** Refusing food or water.
*   **Vomiting or Diarrhea:** General gastrointestinal distress.

*Note: Symptoms may not appear immediately; some signs of anemia can take several days to manifest.*

## What to Do if Your Somali Ate Garlic
If you suspect your cat has ingested garlic or a food containing garlic powder, **treat it as a medical emergency.**

1.  **Remove the Source:** Immediately take away any remaining food to prevent further consumption.
2.  **Call Your Vet:** Contact your veterinarian or an emergency animal clinic immediately.
3.  **Provide Details:** Be ready to tell the vet **how much** garlic was eaten, **when** it happened, and if it was concentrated (like garlic powder).
4.  **Avoid Home Remedies:** Do not attempt to induce vomiting unless specifically instructed to do so by a licensed veterinary professional.

### Safe Alternatives for Your Somali
If you want to give your Somali cat a savory treat or a scent-stimulating experience, stick to cat-safe options:
*   **Catnip or Silvervine:** Great for mental stimulation.
*   **Small pieces of cooked salmon or chicken:** (Plain, no seasoning).
*   **Commercial cat treats:** Formulated specifically for feline nutrition.
