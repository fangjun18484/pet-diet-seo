# Can Cardigan Welsh Corgis Eat Grapes? 🍇

**Verdict: ❌ NO**

## Why You Should Never Feed Grapes to Your Corgi
Regardless of the breed, **grapes and raisins are highly toxic to dogs**, including Cardigan Welsh Corgis. 

Recent veterinary research suggests that **tartaric acid** in grapes is the primary cause of toxicity. Unlike some foods that are only dangerous in large quantities, grapes can cause sudden, acute kidney failure in some dogs, even in small amounts. Because every dog's sensitivity differs, there is no "safe" dose.

---

## The Risks: What Happens if They Eat Grapes?
Feeding your Cardigan Welsh Corgi a grape or raisin can lead to severe health complications. Watch for these warning signs:

### 🚩 Immediate Symptoms (Within hours)
*   **Vomiting:** Often the first sign of distress.
*   **Lethargy:** Unusual tiredness or lack of energy.
*   **Loss of Appetite:** Refusing their favorite treats or meals.
*   **Diarrhea:** Gastrointestinal upset.

### ⚠️ Severe Complications (Long-term)
*   **Acute Kidney Failure:** The kidneys stop filtering waste from the blood.
*   **Dehydration:** Inability to urinate or maintain fluid balance.
*   **Death:** If left untreated, grape toxicity can be fatal.

---

## What to Do If Your Corgi Ate a Grape
If your Cardigan Welsh Corgi accidentally ingested a grape or raisin, **time is of the essence.** Follow these steps immediately:

1.  **Contact Your Vet Immediately:** Call your primary veterinarian or the nearest emergency animal hospital.
2.  **Call a Pet Poison Helpline:** If your vet is unavailable, contact the *ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center* or *Pet Poison Helpline*.
3.  **Do Not Induce Vomiting (Unless Instructed):** Only induce vomiting if a licensed veterinary professional tells you to do so over the phone.
4.  **Provide Information:** Be ready to tell the vet how many grapes were eaten, the approximate time of ingestion, and your dog's current weight.

---

## Safe, Corgi-Friendly Alternatives
Want to give your Corgi a fruity treat? Swap the grapes for these dog-safe, low-calorie options:

*   **Blueberries:** Packed with antioxidants and safe in moderation.
*   **Apple Slices:** (Remove all seeds and the core first).
*   **Watermelon:** (Remove seeds and rind).
*   **Banana:** A great source of potassium, but feed in small amounts.
