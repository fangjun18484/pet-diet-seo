# Can a Cardigan Welsh Corgi Eat Cheese?

**Verdict: YES (In Moderation)**

While most Cardigan Welsh Corgis can safely enjoy a small piece of cheese, it should be treated as an occasional snack rather than a dietary staple.

## Why Can Corgis Eat Cheese?
Cheese is not toxic to dogs. It provides a burst of protein and calcium, and because of its strong aroma and taste, it is an excellent high-value reward for training your Corgi. However, many dogs are **lactose intolerant**, meaning they lack the enzyme (lactase) needed to break down the sugars in dairy.

## The Benefits and Risks

### Potential Benefits
*   **High-Value Training Reward:** Perfect for motivating stubborn Corgis during training sessions.
*   **Nutritional Boost:** Provides small amounts of calcium, phosphorus, and protein.

### Potential Risks
*   **Weight Gain:** Cardigan Welsh Corgis are prone to obesity. Cheese is calorie-dense and can quickly lead to unhealthy weight gain if overfed.
*   **Digestive Upset:** Lactose intolerance can cause gas, bloating, or diarrhea.
*   **Sodium Content:** High salt levels in some cheeses can be hard on a dog's kidneys.
*   **Toxicity Warnings:** Never feed cheese containing **garlic, onion, or chives**, as these are toxic to dogs.

## How to Safely Feed Cheese to Your Corgi

If you want to share a treat with your Cardigan Welsh Corgi, follow these guidelines to keep them healthy:

### 1. Choose the Right Type
Avoid "processed" cheese slices. Instead, opt for:
*   **Hard Cheeses:** Cheddar, Parmesan, or Swiss (these generally have lower lactose levels).
*   **Fresh Cheeses:** Low-sodium cottage cheese or plain Greek yogurt.

### 2. Keep Portions Tiny
A "treat" should be the size of a small die. Your Corgi should only receive cheese as a small fraction of their daily caloric intake.

### 3. Start Slow
If it is your dog's first time eating cheese, give them a tiny piece and monitor them for 24 hours for any signs of stomach distress.

### 4. Avoid "Fancy" Flavors
Avoid any cheese infused with herbs, spices, or artificial sweeteners (like Xylitol), which can be dangerous.

***

**Pro Tip:** *If your Corgi has a sensitive stomach or is overweight, consider using freeze-dried liver or small pieces of carrot as a healthier, lower-calorie alternative for training!*
