# Can a Scottish Fold Eat Grapes?

**Verdict: ❌ NO**

No, a Scottish Fold—or any breed of cat—should **never** eat grapes or raisins.

---

### Why Grapes Are Dangerous for Cats
While grapes are a healthy snack for humans, they are highly toxic to felines. Although the exact compound causing the toxicity is still being researched, it is widely known that grapes and raisins can trigger **acute kidney failure** in cats. 

Because Scottish Folds are already predisposed to certain genetic joint and cartilage issues, maintaining optimal organ health is critical. Introducing a toxin that attacks the kidneys can lead to a rapid and severe health crisis.

---

### Potential Risks & Symptoms
Even a single grape or raisin can be dangerous depending on the size of the cat and its individual sensitivity. If your Scottish Fold consumes grapes, watch for these warning signs:

*   **Vomiting:** Often the first sign of distress.
*   **Lethargy:** Unusual tiredness or lack of energy.
*   **Loss of Appetite:** Refusing their favorite treats or meals.
*   **Dehydration:** Increased thirst or, conversely, a decrease in urine output (a sign of kidney failure).
*   **Diarrhea:** Gastrointestinal upset.

---

### What to Do If Your Cat Ate a Grape
If you suspect your Scottish Fold has ingested a grape or raisin, **treat it as a medical emergency.** Do not wait for symptoms to appear, as kidney damage can happen quickly.

1.  **Call Your Vet Immediately:** Contact your primary veterinarian or the nearest emergency animal clinic.
2.  **Provide Details:** Tell the vet how many grapes were eaten and approximately how long ago the ingestion occurred.
3.  **Do Not Induce Vomiting:** Never attempt to make your cat vomit at home unless specifically instructed to do so by a professional.
4.  **Monitor Water Intake:** Ensure they have access to fresh water while you wait for professional guidance.

### Safe Alternatives for Your Scottish Fold
If you want to give your cat a fruity treat, stick to these cat-safe options in very small quantities:
*   **Blueberries** (Rich in antioxidants)
*   **Cranberries** (Good for urinary tract health)
*   **Pumpkin** (Great for digestion)
