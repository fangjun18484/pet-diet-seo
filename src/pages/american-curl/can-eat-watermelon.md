# Can American Curls Eat Watermelon? 🍉

**Verdict: YES** (In moderation)

### Why Can American Curls Eat Watermelon?
Watermelon is non-toxic to cats, including the American Curl. While cats are obligate carnivores and do not require fruit in their diet, a small piece of watermelon is safe and generally well-tolerated as an occasional treat.

---

### Potential Benefits & Risks

#### ✅ The Benefits
*   **Hydration Boost:** Watermelon is roughly 92% water, making it a great way to provide extra fluids for cats who don't drink enough water.
*   **Low Calorie:** Compared to commercial cat treats, a small cube of watermelon is low in calories.
*   **Vitamin Content:** It contains small amounts of vitamins A and C.

#### ⚠️ The Risks
*   **High Sugar Content:** Watermelon contains natural sugars (fructose). Too much can lead to weight gain or spike blood glucose levels, which is dangerous for cats with **diabetes**.
*   **Digestive Upset:** Some cats may experience diarrhea or stomach upset if they eat too much fruit.
*   **Choking Hazards:** Seeds and the tough outer rind can cause intestinal blockages or choking.

---

### How to Safely Serve Watermelon to Your Cat

If you want to share a slice of summer with your American Curl, follow these safety guidelines:

1.  **Remove All Seeds:** Ensure every seed (black or white) is removed. Seeds are a choking hazard and can cause gastrointestinal blockages.
2.  **Discard the Rind:** Never feed your cat the green skin or the white rind. These are difficult to digest and can cause vomiting or blockages.
3.  **Small Portions Only:** Serve a piece no larger than a **small cube (about 1 cm)**. Treat it as a rare snack, not a meal replacement.
4.  **Fresh is Best:** Only feed fresh watermelon. Avoid canned versions or watermelon-flavored treats, as these often contain added sugars or artificial sweeteners (like Xylitol) that are toxic to cats.

**Pro Tip:** Always introduce new foods slowly. If your American Curl shows any signs of an allergic reaction or digestive distress, remove the treat immediately and consult your veterinarian.
