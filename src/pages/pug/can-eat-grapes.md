# Can a Pug Eat Grapes? The Essential Safety Guide

**Verdict: ❌ NO**

**Pugs cannot eat grapes. Grapes and raisins are highly toxic to dogs, regardless of the breed.**

---

## Why Are Grapes Dangerous for Pugs?
While many fruits are healthy treats, grapes and raisins contain specific compounds (recently linked to **tartaric acid**) that can cause sudden, acute **kidney failure** in dogs. 

Because Pugs are small-breed dogs, even a small amount of grapes can put a significant strain on their renal system. Toxicity can occur regardless of whether the grape is red, green, seedless, or dried.

---

## Potential Risks & Symptoms
Feeding a Pug grapes is not just a digestive risk; it is a medical emergency. There are **no nutritional benefits** to grapes for dogs—only severe risks.

### Warning Signs of Grape Poisoning:
If your Pug has ingested a grape, watch for these critical symptoms:
*   **Vomiting and Diarrhea:** Often occurring within a few hours.
*   **Lethargy:** Unusual tiredness or weakness.
*   **Loss of Appetite:** Refusing their favorite treats or meals.
*   **Dehydration:** Dry gums or sunken eyes.
*   **Decreased Urination:** A sign that the kidneys are beginning to fail.

---

## What to Do If Your Pug Ate a Grape
If your Pug accidentally swallowed a grape or raisin, **time is of the essence.** Do not wait for symptoms to appear, as kidney damage can happen quickly.

1.  **Call Your Vet Immediately:** Contact your primary veterinarian or the nearest emergency animal clinic.
2.  **Provide Details:** Tell the vet how many grapes were eaten, the approximate time of ingestion, and your Pug's current weight.
3.  **Do Not Induce Vomiting Unless Instructed:** Only induce vomiting if your vet specifically tells you to do so over the phone.
4.  **Seek Immediate Treatment:** The vet may administer activated charcoal or IV fluids to help flush the toxins from your dog's system.

### Safe Alternatives for Your Pug
Looking for a crunchy, sweet treat? Try these Pug-safe options instead:
*   **Blueberries** (Rich in antioxidants)
*   **Apple Slices** (Seedless and skinless)
*   **Watermelon** (Seedless and rind-free)
*   **Carrot Sticks** (Great for dental health)
