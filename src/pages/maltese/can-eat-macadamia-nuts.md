# Can a Maltese Eat Macadamia Nuts? 🚫

**Verdict: NO.**

**Maltese dogs cannot eat macadamia nuts.** You should never feed macadamia nuts to your Maltese, nor should you allow them to scavenge them from the counter or floor.

---

## Why are Macadamia Nuts Dangerous for Maltese?
Macadamia nuts contain a specific toxin (though the exact compound is not fully understood) that targets a dog's nervous system. Because Maltese are a **toy breed** with a small body mass, even a single nut can cause a significant toxic reaction compared to a larger dog.

## Potential Risks & Symptoms
If your Maltese consumes macadamia nuts, they may experience **macadamia nut toxicosis**. Symptoms usually appear within 12 hours and can include:

*   **Lethargy:** Unusual tiredness or weakness in the hind legs.
*   **Vomiting:** Frequent nausea or stomach upset.
*   **Hyperthermia:** An increase in body temperature (fever).
*   **Tremors:** In severe cases, muscle tremors or disorientation.
*   **Loss of Appetite:** Refusing their favorite treats or meals.

**Note:** While these symptoms often resolve on their own within 24–48 hours, they can be distressing and potentially dangerous for a small dog.

---

## What to Do if Your Maltese Ate a Macadamia Nut
If you suspect your Maltese has ingested macadamia nuts, **do not wait for symptoms to appear.** Follow these steps immediately:

1.  **Remove Access:** Ensure no more nuts are available to your dog.
2.  **Call Your Veterinarian:** Contact your vet or an emergency animal clinic immediately. 
3.  **Provide Details:** Tell the vet roughly how many nuts were eaten and how long ago it happened.
4.  **Monitor Closely:** Watch for the symptoms listed above. Do **not** induce vomiting unless specifically instructed to do so by a veterinary professional.

### Safe Alternatives for Your Maltese
If you want to give your Maltese a healthy, crunchy snack, try these dog-safe options instead:
*   **Blueberries** (Antioxidant-rich)
*   **Apple slices** (Without seeds)
*   **Carrot sticks** (Great for dental health)
*   **Plain cooked pumpkin** (Good for digestion)
