# Can a Bernese Mountain Dog Eat Macadamia Nuts?

**Verdict: ❌ NO**

## Why Macadamia Nuts Are Dangerous for Dogs
Regardless of their size, **Bernese Mountain Dogs should never eat macadamia nuts.** These nuts contain a toxin (the exact nature of which is still being studied, but it is highly potent) that affects a dog's neurological system. 

While a large breed like a Bernese Mountain Dog may be more resilient to a single small piece than a toy breed, the risk of toxicity is too high to ignore. Macadamia nuts are among the most toxic foods for canines.

---

## Potential Risks & Symptoms
If your Bernese Mountain Dog consumes macadamia nuts, they may experience **Macadamia Nut Toxicosis**. Symptoms typically appear within 12 hours and can include:

*   **Hind Limb Weakness:** Difficulty walking or "stumbling" (ataxia).
*   **Lethargy:** Extreme tiredness or depression.
*   **Vomiting:** Frequent nausea and stomach upset.
*   **Hyperthermia:** An increase in body temperature.
*   **Tremors:** In severe cases, muscle tremors or seizures may occur.

**Are there any benefits?** 
No. There are no nutritional benefits to macadamia nuts that outweigh the severe risks of toxicity.

---

## What to Do If Your Dog Ate Macadamia Nuts
If you suspect your Bernese Mountain Dog has ingested macadamia nuts, **act quickly**.

1.  **Call Your Veterinarian Immediately:** Contact your vet or an emergency animal clinic.
2.  **Contact Pet Poison Control:** Call the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center or a similar service.
3.  **Identify the Quantity:** Try to determine exactly how many nuts were eaten and when.
4.  **Monitor Symptoms:** Keep a close eye on their movement and energy levels while transporting them to the clinic.
5.  **Do Not Induce Vomiting** unless specifically instructed to do so by a veterinary professional.

## Safe, Crunchy Alternatives
If your Berner loves a crunch, swap the dangerous nuts for these dog-safe options:
*   **Carrot Sticks:** Low calorie and great for dental health.
*   **Apple Slices:** (Pith and seeds removed).
*   **Cucumber:** A hydrating, low-calorie treat.
*   **Plain Frozen Blueberries:** A nutrient-dense antioxidant snack.
